## Are there artist residencies with no application fee?

Yes. Some residencies take applications for free, but check the current cycle's rules every time.

The [Edward F. Albee Foundation](https://apply.albeefoundation.org/) is a clear example: its application page says there's no fee of any kind, from application through residency. Its next window for writers and visual artists runs January 11 to February 15, 2027, so it's a model to know, not a call you can enter today.

A fee waiver is a different route, and you have to qualify or ask before you pay. MacDowell charges no residency fee but a [US$30 processing fee](https://www.macdowell.org/apply/application-guidelines) to apply, and you can request a hardship waiver after you start the application and before you submit it. Don't assume a waiver from a past cycle still applies.

Start with [residencies with no application fee](/discover/residencies-no-fee), then open the organizer's page. Look for a plain zero on the form itself, and check for any required membership. Missa cards show the fee, "No fee", or "Fee not listed" when the organizer's page doesn't say. Treat "not listed" as unknown until you get an answer.

## What does "fully funded" usually cover?

"Fully funded" means the organizer pays for a stated package, and only that program's written terms tell you what's in it.

Turn the phrase into an itemized budget. Housing, a studio and meals help, but they don't mean your flights, materials or bills at home are paid. A cash stipend might cover living costs, might have rent taken out, or might arrive only after you get there.

[RESONANCE](https://www.artxlagos.com/resonance) shows the gap. Its closed 2026–2027 call described a three-month Paris residency with a live-in studio, a monthly allowance and travel to Paris. The visa and insurance stayed with the artist. A strong package can still need money upfront.

Use the table to turn each line into a question for the organizer:

| Budget item | What "covered" should mean | What to ask |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Application | Nothing to pay to enter, or a confirmed waiver | Is there a charge on the form, or a required membership? |
| Housing | Somewhere to live for the whole required stay | Are utilities, deposits and arrival nights included? |
| Studio | Suitable space you can actually use | Is equipment included? Are there usage charges? |
| Stipend | An amount, a currency and a payment date | Is rent deducted? When does the first payment reach me? |
| Travel | Named trips, with any spending cap | Do you book tickets, or pay me back afterward? |
| Visa and permits | Named fees paid, or real help with documents | Who pays the fees and supplies the paperwork? |
| Meals | A set meal plan or allowance | Which days and meals are covered? |
| Materials | A production budget you can use | What counts, and do I need approval first? |
| Access and family needs | An arrangement that meets your needs | Can you support an assistant, dependents or accessible housing? |

## What costs can a free residency leave out?

A residency with no attendance fee can still leave you paying for travel, materials, insurance, family costs and lost income.

List the cost of saying yes next to the support on offer, including the bills at home that keep running while you're away. A residency can give you a bedroom and still be out of reach if you have to stop paid work with nothing to replace it.

Then check the order of payments. If travel is reimbursed at the end, you have to buy the ticket first. If the stipend starts days after you arrive, you need money for food and local transport until it lands. The Albee Foundation is a real case. Its US$2,500 stipend for domestic fellows is paid after the residency, and so is its expense reimbursement for international fellows, capped at US$2,500 and paid against proof of costs.

Here's the arithmetic with invented numbers, not a typical award. Say a residency offers a US$1,000 stipend. If travel costs US$600 and insurance and documents another US$150, you have US$250 left before materials or home bills. Do the same sum with the organizer's real figures.

When money crosses borders, ask about conversion and transfer charges, and budget from the organizer's payment terms, not an exchange rate you haven't checked. If a fixed travel grant might fall short, get a current fare quote before you decide you can go.

> [!WARNING]
> Ask when the money arrives, not just how much. A stipend or reimbursement paid after the stay won't pay for the ticket there.

## Do any residencies pay a stipend?

Yes, some do. A stipend is money paid to you, and it isn't the same thing as free housing or a free application.

Read the money section closely. Programs call it a living allowance, honorarium, bursary or grant. Find out how much you can actually use, what it's meant to cover, and what gets deducted.

[Jan van Eyck Academie](https://www.janvaneyck.nl/residency/apply) in Maastricht shows why. Its listed stipend is €1,500 a month, but rent for the studio apartment comes out of it, and there's a €2,750 enrollment fee. [MacDowell](https://www.macdowell.org/apply/financial-assistance) offers need-based stipends of up to US$1,500, set by the length of your stay, and you can only apply for one once you've been awarded a fellowship.

Don't assume everyone selected gets the same support. A scholarship might cut a fee instead of paying you cash, and a production budget might cover materials only, against receipts. If the terms mix several benefits, ask for a worked example: "this amount, minus this rent" is a different deal from housing plus a stipend you can spend freely.

For a wider shortlist, browse [current residency calls](/discover/residencies), then go back to each organizer's page for the actual package.

## What should I ask before I accept?

Before you say yes, get written answers to the questions that decide whether you can actually go.

- [ ] What exactly does the funding cover, item by item?
- [ ] How much is the stipend, in what currency, and when is the first payment?
- [ ] Is rent or anything else deducted from it?
- [ ] Do you book travel, or pay me back afterward? Is there a cap?
- [ ] Who pays for the visa, permits and insurance?
- [ ] Which meals are covered, and on which days?
- [ ] Is there a materials budget, and what does it cover?
- [ ] Can you meet my access needs, or support a child, partner or assistant?
- [ ] What happens if my visa is delayed or I need to move my dates?

## Are free residencies harder to get into?

You can't tell from the fee. A free application says nothing on its own about how selective a residency is.

A well-funded program may get a lot of applications, but who you're up against depends on its discipline, residence rules, selection criteria and number of places. A small local call and an international residency differ in far more than the fee.

Read the eligibility rules first, then ask whether your samples and proposal fit. A tough field calls for a balanced shortlist and strong materials, not for skipping a program because it's free or funded. ## How do I search without mixing up free entry and funded attendance?

Filter for no application fee first. Then go back through your shortlist and read each program's money terms.

The first pass answers one question: can you enter without paying? The second records what support you'd get and what you'd still pay if selected. Give those separate columns, so a "free" label can't hide a stay you can't afford.

Check the application cycle and the residency dates together. A page might be taking applications for a stay more than a year away, and a program you like might be closed while it picks its residents. Keep closed programs as calendar research, not open calls.

On Missa, a no-fee residency result is where you start checking, not proof of a full funding package or visa eligibility. Every call links to the organizer's page, and you apply there. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is a free artist residency the same as a fully funded one?

No. "Free" may describe the application or the stay. "Fully funded" should come with an itemized list of what's covered and what isn't.

### Should I ask for a fee waiver before applying?

Yes, if the rules offer one and you need it. Check who qualifies and how to ask before you pay, and don't count on a refund later.

### Does a stipend cover travel?

Only if the terms say so. Travel can be separate, capped, reimbursed later or left to you, so confirm how it's paid before you book.

### Can I bring a child, partner or support person?

Sometimes. Jan van Eyck Academie, for one, lists a larger stipend and apartment for participants with children. Ask each program directly about housing, access and extra costs, because a general welcome isn't the same as permission or funding.
