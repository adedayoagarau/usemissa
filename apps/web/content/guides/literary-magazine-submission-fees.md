## Why do literary magazines charge submission fees?

Magazines charge to help pay for reading, admin and publishing, but only the magazine itself can tell you what its fee pays for.

[AGNI’s guidelines](https://agnionline.bu.edu/submit/) say its US$3 online reading fee helps pay the editors who read your work. AGNI also offers fee waivers, and work sent on paper costs nothing to submit. That’s AGNI’s reason. Don’t assume it applies to every magazine with an online form.

A free account and a free submission are two different things. The submission manager may let you sign up for free while the magazine charges for a particular category. Look at the actual form, including any required add-ons, before you confirm.

A published reason makes a fee easier to judge. It doesn’t mean you have to pay it. You can read and support a magazine without paying every reading fee it offers.

## Is it normal to pay to submit to a literary magazine?

Some magazines charge reading fees, but many read general submissions for free, and you never have to use a paid route.

[The Kenyon Review](https://kenyonreview.org/submit/) says it doesn’t charge a reading fee for general submissions. [Poetry](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/poetrymagazine/submit) says submitting is free. Neither means every contest or special category elsewhere is free.

Duotrope’s [fee advice for editors](https://duotrope.com/duosuma/docs/fee-recommendations.aspx) says the most common fee among the few journals that charge one is US$3, and that anything from US$2 to US$5 is in the average range. That’s Duotrope’s reading of its own listings, not an industry standard or a judgment of value.

Start by working out what kind of payment you’re looking at. “Varies” means we couldn’t find a reliable general range.

| Fee type | Typical amount | What it should get you | Fair when | Think twice when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| General reading fee | Varies; [AGNI](https://agnionline.bu.edu/submit/) charges US$3 online | Consideration under published rules | The magazine fits and the terms are clear | It’s presented as a way to get accepted |
| Expedited response | Varies | A faster reply in a stated timeframe | You have a real scheduling need | It implies better treatment, or refunds are unclear |
| Optional tip jar | Your choice, including nothing | The standard submission, without donating | You want to support the magazine | There’s no working way to submit for free |
| Contest entry | Varies by contest | Entry into a defined competition | Prize, judge, eligibility, rights and dates are clear | The prize or later costs are unclear |
| Free reading period | US$0 during that window | Standard consideration | The window is open for your category | A payment shows up before you finish |

## When is a contest entry fee fair?

A contest fee is easiest to judge when the organizer publishes the prize, how it’s judged, who can enter, the rights and the full cost before you pay.

Read the contest rules on their own, apart from the magazine’s regular guidelines. Check what work qualifies, how finalists are picked, what happens if the contest is canceled and whether the winner is guaranteed publication. Find out about any required book purchase or later charge.

Judge a prize on terms you can check. A big advertised award isn’t enough if the eligibility rules leave you out or the rights terms don’t work for you. The work should fit the category, not be squeezed in because the prize looks good.

Compare the contest with sending the same work to no-fee magazines, where that’s allowed. If what you want is publication, not the prize itself, that alternative matters. If you do enter, log the fee, currency, deadline and contract terms with the submission.

Browse [free contests](/discover/free-contests) for alternatives, and check [chapbook contests](/discover/chapbook-contests) for manuscript rules. A book-length contest has different preparation and publication terms from a single-poem competition.

## What’s a tip jar, and is it really optional?

A tip jar is an optional donation. If you can’t submit without paying, it’s a fee, whatever it’s called.

Check the form for a working way to submit the same category without donating. Read what the magazine says about reading order, response times and how submissions are treated. A separate paid service should be labeled as one, not tucked inside the tip jar.

If the form isn’t clear, ask the magazine whether you can submit without paying and how. Don’t pay first and count on a refund. Save their answer or the instructions for next time.

A donation should be its own decision about supporting the magazine. You shouldn’t have to guess whether donating helps your result. If a magazine says tips change how work is read, treat that as a paid service and decide whether you want it.

Missa’s “No fee” and “Fee not listed” labels separate a stated fee from missing information. Still check the form itself: a listing can’t cover every add-on or category.

## How do I find magazines that read for free?

Start with no-fee listings, then check the magazine’s current categories and reading window on its own site.

Open [magazine calls](/discover/magazines) and sort by “Free to apply first,” or start from [no-fee calls](/discover/no-fee-calls) and pick out the magazines. Check that the magazine takes your genre and that the no-fee statement covers the exact category you’re sending.

Some magazines have free periods at certain times, waivers on request or free entry for subscribers. A subscription still costs money, so treat it as buying something to read that comes with a submission perk. It isn’t free for anyone who doesn’t already subscribe.

Mail can have no reading fee and still cost you printing and postage. Compare that total with the online fee, especially if you’re mailing internationally. Check that the magazine accepts paper submissions before you send anything.

> [!TIP]
> When a free window has closed, put the next one in your calendar and have the work ready before it opens. You won’t have to rush the final draft.

## How much should I budget for submissions in a year?

Set a limit you can afford without a single acceptance, and log every fee as you pay it.

Here’s a made-up budget, not an average writer’s spending. Twenty general submissions at US$3 each come to US$60. Two contests at US$20 each add US$40. That’s US$100 in fees before subscriptions, postage or anything else. Small fees add up.

Pick a yearly or quarterly limit, then split it across the things you actually value. A tracker subscription and a submission fee are separate costs. Give tips and workshops their own lines too, so they don’t vanish into a general writing budget.

Look back at your spending now and then. Note which fees didn’t lead to a useful experience and decide whether to repeat them. A rejection doesn’t make a fee unfair. A hidden charge or a service that never arrived is a different problem.

If your budget is zero, build your list from confirmed free routes. You can still make a long list without treating every familiar magazine as a must-pay.

## What should I check before paying?

Check four things before you pay: the fit, the full charge, the submission terms and where the money is going.

- [ ] Read recent work in the magazine
- [ ] Check the genre and length limits
- [ ] Check whether it’s a regular submission or a contest
- [ ] Read the rights and simultaneous-submission policy
- [ ] Check the final amount and currency at checkout
- [ ] Confirm who you’re paying
- [ ] Keep the receipt

If you’re not sure who the organizer is or where the payment is going, use the [scam-check guide](/guides/open-call-scams-vanity-galleries). For exhibition costs, the [guide to entry and participation fees](/guides/entry-fees-hanging-fees-participation-fees) separates what you pay before and after selection.

## Frequently asked questions

### Does a submission fee improve my chance of acceptance?

Not by itself. A standard fee pays for the consideration the rules describe. It doesn’t guarantee editorial interest or acceptance.

### Is an optional tip jar a no-fee submission?

Yes, if you can finish the submission without donating. Check the actual form and category.

### Are fee waivers available?

Some magazines offer them. AGNI does, on request. Follow each magazine’s own process before you pay.
