# Script moves

These are structures taken from real commercial scripts, not templates to fill in.
Each draft should use one. The examples come from Edge Studio's free voice-over
script library, quoted briefly for study.

## Wry, confident opener with a turn

Start with a claim the audience half-believes, undercut it in a few words, then turn
it into your point. It works on a sceptical audience that has heard every promise.

- Beringer: *"Some might choose a lecture… We prefer a glass."*
- Harvey: *"He performs cardiac surgery for a living… Now, if he could only operate
  the microwave."*
- Missa: *"Some people will tell you the art world runs on talent. / Talent helps. /
  But mostly, it runs on deadlines."*

How it works: the second line must be **short and dry**, and the payoff at the end
calls back to the opener ("Talent's your department. Deadlines are ours.").

## First person

The viewer hears someone they recognise describe their own life. It suits community
products, and the person must sound real, not narrated.

- Revlon: *"Funny thing being a redhead…"*
- Missa draft: *"I found out about the residency from a friend's Instagram story. The
  day after it closed."*

How it works: give the speaker one embarrassing, specific detail. It needs a voice
that sounds like the character, often a custom or designed voice.

## Named character

A third-person story about one person, told in a few strokes. It suits warmth and
causes.

- GiveForward: *"Jeff recently found out he is sick."*
- Missa draft: *"Tolu paints. Beautifully. She has a studio full of finished work, a
  phone full of screenshots of open calls, and a calendar with nothing in it."*

How it works: the name and the very first detail carry the whole thing. Don't
invent statistics about the character that read as real data.

## Sustained metaphor

One image carried from the first line to the last. It suits brand films and
manifestos.

- 4-H: seed → took root → *"True leaders aren't born. They're grown."*
- Missa draft: doors. *"Every artist remembers a door that closed on them. Very few
  ever find out how many were open."*

How it works: never mix in a second metaphor, and the product sentence has to fit
the image ("finds the doors that fit your work").

## Countdown or rhythm

Repetition is the hook. It suits short cutdowns and urgency.

- Missa draft: *"Fourteen days. Seven. Three. One. Today."*

How it works: the rhythm must carry meaning (here, real reminder offsets), not just
sound good. It is easy to slide into a feature list.

## Notes from the library

- Most scripts in the library are amateur: long, descriptive, and full of
  adjectives. Spend your time on the few that make one of these moves.
- The strong ones share three habits:
  - a specific moment or person in the first line;
  - one idea per line;
  - a last line that pays off the first.
