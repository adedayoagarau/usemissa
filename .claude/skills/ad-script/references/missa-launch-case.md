# Case study: the Missa launch script

**Film:** `video/launch`, PR #145. Brief: announce Missa as the opportunity layer for
every creator.

## Round 1: rejected ("robotic")

> Grants. Residencies. Prizes. Open calls.
> They're scattered across the internet, each with its own deadline, fee and rules.
> Missa brings them together.
> Compare the facts. Open the official source. Save your decision. Track what comes next.
> For writers, painters, filmmakers and musicians.
> Missa. The opportunity layer for every creator.

What was wrong with it:

- **Too narrow.** The creator list named four disciplines; Missa serves six worlds.
- **Missed the main job.** It said nothing about deadline management, which is the
  heart of the product.
- **UI copy as voiceover.** The four verbs are the homepage's button language.
- **"Opportunity layer"** is investor language.
- **Robotic picture.** The film used numbered scene counters ("01 / 04").

## Round 2: rejected ("tech-led", "AI to AI")

The drafts added deadlines, but they still described the product. The best of them
opened on a story ("You found it on a Tuesday… It closed on Friday"), then fell
back into "Missa finds the call, shows you the facts and the official source, then
keeps the date for you."

The feedback, which became step 3 of the skill:

> "Hook! Use hook! Lead with why many artists have not made so much progress. Many
> don't know where to find opportunities. Some find it but they actually miss out on
> deadlines… This is an ad to real people, not an AI to AI conversation."

## Round 3: rejected ("slop")

The drafts led with the problem ("Somewhere out there is a grant with your name on
it. You'll probably never see it."). They were better, but generic: they sounded
like every other ad, and nothing in them was specific to Missa. The user pointed to
Edge Studio's script library, which became step 4.

## Round 4: chosen

After reading about 375 real scripts, the drafts were rebuilt on four named moves.
The wry opener won:

> Some people will tell you the art world runs on talent.
> Talent helps.
> But mostly, it runs on deadlines.
> Grant deadlines. Residency deadlines. That magazine that only reads submissions in March.
> Miss one, and you wait a year.
> Missa finds the opportunities that fit what you make, and reminds you before every one of them closes.
> Talent's your department. Deadlines are ours.
> Missa. Opportunities for every creator.

Why it works:

- **Starts from a belief the audience holds** (talent), and agrees with it before
  turning it.
- **"Talent helps."** is two words of dry restraint. That beat sets the voice of the
  whole film.
- **The specific detail does the persuading.** "That magazine that only reads
  submissions in March" makes the problem concrete.
- **The stakes come in five words:** "Miss one, and you wait a year."
- **The product gets one sentence**, in plain verbs, and every claim in it is
  shipped (matching on what you make, and reminders before each deadline).
- **The payoff splits the work:** "Talent's your department. Deadlines are ours."
  It is a callback, not a slogan pair, because each half answers the opener.
- **It closes on the homepage line,** not a new tagline.

How it carried into production:

- **Delivery tags:** `[dry]`, `[short pause]`, `[knowingly]`, `[softly]`,
  `[warmly]`, `[confident]`.
- **Voice:** a voice with wry restraint beat the polished ad narrators.
- **Cutdowns:** the 15s and 6s both open on "But mostly, it runs on deadlines."
