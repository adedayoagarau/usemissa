# Missa social media strategy

September 6, 2026 · Proposed 90-day operating plan

## Strategic direction

Make Missa the account creators return to when they are deciding where to send their work. Lead with useful opportunities; build trust through clear facts; turn discovery into a repeat habit.

Public promise: **Opportunities and grants for every creator**

The user-confirmed lead line is **Opportunities and grants for every creator** Use it consistently across launch creative, profile copy, and brand introductions. Supporting copy: **For writers, visual artists, performers, filmmakers, musicians, and designers.** Primary CTA: **Explore**. This user-supplied copy supersedes the earlier positioning line for this social strategy. Do not introduce a competing campaign slogan.

Success means someone discovers a relevant call, understands the terms, visits Missa, and returns for another useful decision. Follower growth supports that outcome; it is not the outcome itself.

## Audience and channel choices

Working assumption: a small founder-led team, organic launch, and no paid-media budget committed.

Address the audience named in the new direction: writers, visual artists, performers, filmmakers, musicians, and designers. Rotate discipline-specific selections so each post makes its audience explicit. Publication volume should follow verified opportunity supply; do not imply equal catalogue coverage across disciplines. Include global eligibility clearly; build a recurring selection relevant to African and diaspora creators where verified supply supports it.

Instagram is the primary editorial channel because it matches the supplied direction. Use carousels for decisions, Stories for timely updates and links, and original short videos for explanations and human presence. Repurpose one useful weekly insight to the founder's LinkedIn for editors, arts organizations, and potential partners. Defer additional channels until production and referrals are consistent. This is a capacity decision, not an algorithm claim.

## Visual system

Use the current repository identity: Forest-led color, Newsreader for editorial moments, Instrument Sans for clear headlines and body text, Fragment Mono for dates and metadata. Use the existing official wordmark asset; do not typeset a substitute.

The photographic direction should feel intimate, tactile, and editorial. Use close crops of people, hands, clothing, tools, work surfaces, and work in progress. Deep green can enter through fabric, a setting, or a flat supporting panel; preserve natural skin and material colors. Avoid a blanket green filter. Use licensed or permissioned images and do not imply a stock model is a Missa user or fellowship recipient.

Proposed template family:

1. Opportunity: a relevant photograph with a brief serif headline in clear negative space, followed by quiet white or pale forest fact slides. Use a typographic cover when no truthful, relevant image is available.
2. Shortlist: a photographic or solid forest cover with a specific audience and concise title; keep counts secondary to relevance. Follow with one consistent fact card per call.
3. Explainer: white background, one question per slide, generous space, and short examples. Material or process details may introduce the topic.
4. Editorial: an intimate photographic crop with one short line. The launch cover reads “Opportunities and grants for every creator”
5. Creator: a permissioned portrait or working-process sequence, with restrained identification and credit. Let the person's own account supply the story.

Use Newsreader for the main editorial headlines and Instrument Sans for supporting information. Keep the official white wordmark visible but secondary to the photograph and message. Use image-specific placement to protect faces and important work details. Place text on a quiet area or a separate panel when contrast is insufficient.

Keep category names in text rather than relying on color. Design each post to work on its own; avoid a grid composition that depends on adjacent posts. Start with portrait carousel masters and vertical video/Story masters, then preview actual feed, profile crops, and interface overlays before exporting. Check contrast, phone-size legibility, alt text, and video captions.

Opening sequence before the regular calendar:

1. Brand introduction: photographic cover, “Opportunities and grants for every creator” Supporting slides name the creative disciplines and explain what people can find.
2. A useful shortlist: audience-specific, verified calls with eligibility, costs, and deadlines.
3. How Missa helps: a concise walkthrough of currently available discovery, with the same photographic opening and clear product screenshots inside.

These establish identity, immediate usefulness, and a next action. Follow with the recurring calendar below; photography should remain a regular part of opportunity covers and creator stories, rather than appearing only in occasional brand posts.

## Opportunity carousel and caption template

1. **Why consider it?** Program name, concrete benefit, discipline, and deadline. Include a major eligibility restriction on the cover when it determines fit.
2. **Who can apply?** Geography, career stage, discipline, and exclusions. International does not mean unrestricted.
3. **What is covered?** Stipend, accommodation, travel, duration, and applicant-paid costs. Distinguish per-person awards from total funding pools.
4. **What will you need?** Work samples, proposal, references, fee, and relevant commitments.
5. **What next?** Exact closing date and stated timezone, official organization, source-check date, and route to full details.

Caption skeleton:

> [Program] is accepting applications from [verified eligible audience].
>
> Support: [specific benefit and limits].
> Application fee: [amount / no fee / not stated].
> Deadline: [date, time, timezone as published].
>
> Worth checking if [fact-based fit consideration].
> Read the requirements through the link in our bio. Official source: [organization and source URL]. Checked [date].

Use one primary CTA. A Story should link directly to the relevant Missa record where available. A feed post should identify the listing clearly enough to find from the bio destination. Do not withhold the name, deadline, or eligibility to force a click.

## Acquisition and return loop

Post → relevant Missa listing or filtered selection → official requirements → an available save action → later return.

Use direct, tagged Story links. For feed traffic, use an existing relevant browse destination; if a dedicated social landing page is later built, show the latest posts with clear listing titles. That page is a proposed follow-up, not part of this strategy's implementation.

Promote saving only after checking the live signed-in journey. Until then, use “Read the details.” Repository notes show ongoing release verification; they do not establish every production capability. Do not promise automatic submission, working notifications, synced calendars, or response predictions based on prototypes. An official-source click is not a completed application.

## Editorial operations and trust

Maintain one production sheet with: post ID, series, audience, opportunity ID, official URL, deadline/timezone, fee, funding breakdown, eligibility, source-check timestamp, reviewer, asset rights, caption, destination URL, campaign tags, publication date, and expiry/correction status.

Select candidates from Missa's publishable catalogue, then check the official program page immediately before scheduling and again on publication day. Exclude unresolved critical deadline or eligibility conflicts. Label noncritical unknowns plainly. Source-first records are the starting point; being in a database does not prove freshness.

Prefer opportunities with enough preparation time. Label late discoveries honestly. Do not use “confirmed legit,” acceptance guarantees, or giant summed funding totals as default hooks. “Fully funded” requires a defensible breakdown of what is and is not covered. Disclose sponsored placement and retain the same factual checks.

One person researches and drafts; a second reviewer checks dates, money, eligibility, and links where staffing permits. If working alone, perform a separate source-check pass. Review comments each working day. Turn recurring questions into explainers. Answer uncertain eligibility questions with the source and route to the organizer, not an invented ruling.

When a material error is found, pause scheduled derivatives, correct the caption and linked record, and publish a visible correction where the original image could mislead. Update or archive materially wrong assets; record what changed. Mark closed calls clearly and remove them from active selections.

Plan roughly 6–8 staff hours weekly as an initial capacity estimate: research and checking, templated production, scheduling, community replies, and review. Measure actual effort in the first fortnight. Creator interviews may require additional time.

Make original summaries with useful comparison and explanation. Develop a small partner list of literary magazines, artist-run spaces, residency organizers, and creator educators. Propose one co-authored explainer or permissioned creator feature every two weeks once the publishing workflow is stable. Outreach and publishing require a separate execution request; none has been sent under this plan.

Use specific conversation prompts: “Which costs do you need a residency to cover?” or “What information is usually missing from an open call?” Record answers as editorial inputs. Avoid engagement bait and repetitive promotional comments on other accounts.

## Measurement and 90-day decisions

Primary proposed measure: weekly social-attributed visitors who save a relevant opportunity, once live saving and consent-aware attribution are verified. Temporary measure: social-attributed visitors who open opportunity details and then the official source. Neither proves application completion or fit.

| Question | Measure |
| --- | --- |
| Is the content useful enough to keep or send? | Instagram saves/reach and shares/reach, reported separately |
| Does it bring people to Missa? | Tagged sessions and Story link taps; keep platform and site counts distinct |
| Do visitors take a next step? | Unique visitors saving / attributed visitors, or the temporary detail-to-source measure |
| Do they return? | Seven-day return among measurable social-acquired visitors |
| Is the operation trustworthy? | Material correction count, expired active posts, and percent checked on publication day |

Use consistent source, medium, campaign, and post-ID tags. Check which analytics already exist before adding instrumentation. Bio-link attribution is directional because several posts share one link; Instagram saves and Missa saves are different events. Exclude staff/test traffic and avoid collecting sensitive application details for marketing measurement.

Days 1–30: establish identity and a reliable cadence, publish up to 20 posts, and establish baselines. Operational targets: all opportunity posts source-checked on publication day; no expired calls in active highlights. No invented follower or conversion forecast.

Days 31–60: repeat the two series with strongest useful actions and site referrals. Test one variable at a time: benefit-led versus audience-led cover, or carousel versus narrated explanation. Compare similar subjects and acknowledge small samples and different deadlines.

Days 61–90: allocate more slots to the series producing repeat visitors and saves; assess production cost per useful visitor. If reach grows without site action, inspect the CTA and destination. If visits grow without saves, inspect fit and the live save journey. If returning usage remains weak, revise selection and product handoff before increasing volume or buying reach.
