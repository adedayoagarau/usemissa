# Recent design uplifts: three-lens review

Review mode: critique, BUILD intensity. This is one assistant applying three installed skill rubrics, not three independent reviewers or user research. Scores are subjective design judgments, not usability or release certification. No product code changed during this review.

Evidence: refreshed local desktop renders of /opportunities, /directory, /discover/women-nonbinary-opportunities and /discover/contests; a fresh 390px render of the Women & Non-Binary collection. Other collection variants were not individually browser-reviewed in this pass. The audience is creators trying to find relevant opportunities and organizations. The index should prioritize scanning; collection covers can provide editorial identity without obscuring their subject.

## Casey: concept and structural rigor

Primary diagnosis: a metaphor problem in collection covers and a trust problem in result imagery/content. The index structure is stronger than the symbolic vocabulary.

### Seven tests

| Test | Verdict | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stop | Pass for covers; appropriate restraint for indexes | Indigo serif and yellow sans covers command attention. Search-first indexes should not compete for poster-like attention. |
| Compression | Partial | “Take up space” has a useful central idea, but the separate orbital symbol does not express it. |
| Duality | Weak | The orbit is attractive geometry with a science association; it adds little second-level meaning about this collection. |
| Appropriateness | Partial | The contest burst plausibly evokes recognition. The atom-like motif could serve an unrelated science page. |
| Humor | Weak | There is no typographic discovery or visual wit yet. This is an opportunity, not a requirement to add jokes. |
| Reduction | Partial | The About section is gone. The detached decorative symbol and repeated generic Directory descriptions remain removable. |
| Proportion | Mostly pass | Strong grid, consistent card edges and deliberate headline contrast. The mobile cover still consumes much of the first screen before results. |

Keep: white index, common cards and controls, restored organization logos, and the typographic confidence of the collection covers.
Burn: interchangeable symbols and generic fallback sentences that contribute no new information.
Reinvent: the relationship between collection title, graphic and subject.

The following historical precedents are drawn from the Casey skill's supplied reference notes, not independently authenticated museum research.

**Recommendation 1: Make the headline occupy the space**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** The atom-like icon competes with “Take up space” while communicating a different subject.

**The Reframe:** The words already contain the visual concept.

**The Prescription:** Remove the orbit on this cover. Let the Newsreader headline span the available grid and use an expanding typographic interval or deliberately occupied margin to express space, without reducing legibility.

**The Precedent:** The supplied notes describe Body Language as letters becoming the subject.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** A distinctive headline remains attached to a stock-looking science symbol.

**Recommendation 2: Give each motif a subject-specific reason**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** Four motif types are recycled across twelve covers.

**The Reframe:** Different colors do not establish twelve different concepts.

**The Prescription:** Write one sentence explaining what each motif communicates. Retain the contest burst only if developed as a recognition mark; remove any motif that can move unchanged to an unrelated collection.

**The Precedent:** Goya in the supplied notes demonstrates subject compressed into one mark.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** The system becomes a palette picker with decorations.

**Recommendation 3: Restore hierarchy to the collection identity**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** The actual collection name is a small uppercase label while an expressive slogan dominates.

**The Reframe:** Recognition must precede emotional invitation on a destination page.

**The Prescription:** Increase the category label from 11px to 13–14px, reduce tracking, and keep its complete name legible at 390px; retain the expressive headline as the focal point.

**The Precedent:** The structural clarity paired with expressive type in the supplied Casey examples.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** Visitors arriving directly may understand the mood before understanding the page.

**Recommendation 4: Stop repeated photos from pretending to distinguish records**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** Adjacent unrelated opportunities share the same mountain, gallery or artist image.

**The Reframe:** The picture occupies the largest card area but supplies little identifying information.

**The Prescription:** Prefer an opportunity image with established provenance; otherwise use a compact organization identity or an intentional type-based fallback. Do not add random photos merely for variety.

**The Precedent:** The reduction principle illustrated by Goya in the supplied reference.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** The catalogue feels populated by templates rather than distinct opportunities.

**Recommendation 5: Remove empty Directory prose**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** “Explore this artist residency’s profile” repeats the View profile action.

**The Reframe:** Missing information does not need a sentence to fill its space.

**The Prescription:** Omit generic fallback summaries; preserve actual summaries and available practice metadata. Keep footer alignment using layout, not invented prose.

**The Precedent:** Casey's supplied reduction and grid principles.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** Quiet cards become padded rather than informative.

**Recommendation 6: Tune mobile cover depth**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** The cover plus toolbar leaves only the beginning of a card visible at 390px.

**The Reframe:** The cover should introduce the collection and hand over to browsing.

**The Prescription:** Remove the separate 72px mobile artwork column when the symbol carries no necessary meaning; reduce mobile cover gaps and test a cover roughly 300–340px tall with full category name retained.

**The Precedent:** Casey's proportion test: precise structure in service of a human reader.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** Editorial impact delays the page's practical payoff.

**Recommendation 7: Make catalogue integrity part of the visual standard**

**The Casey Diagnosis:** Directory shows duplicate Exclamation Mark Lit profiles side by side; the Contests page includes cards titled as residencies.

**The Reframe:** Visual authority depends on credible distinctions in the records.

**The Prescription:** Audit duplicate identity and contest classification upstream. Verify whether residency calls legitimately contain a contest before changing them. Do not silently hide duplicates in presentation or claim the data is wrong from titles alone.

**The Precedent:** Casey's subject-immersion and appropriateness tests.

**The Danger of Ignoring This:** Better typography makes contradictory content more conspicuous.

## Creative Direction

MODE: CRITIQUE | Intensity: BUILD

VERDICT: SOLID

SCORES:
- Concept strength: 6/10 — a coherent discovery system; cover meanings are only partly resolved.
- Composition and spatial intelligence: 8/10 — strong alignment, clean grids, confident distinction between index and collection.
- Typography: 8/10 — Instrument Sans browsing and Newsreader editorial expression work together.
- Color strategy: 8/10 — the indigo and yellow examples are distinctive and readable; twelve palettes need consistent ownership rules.
- Image/illustration quality: 5/10 — interchangeable motifs and repeated fallback photography weaken specificity.
- Craft and finish: 7/10 — orderly controls and card actions; sparse-content cards and mobile cover depth need refinement.
- Originality and surprise: 6/10 — stronger than the prior plain pages, but the cover remains a familiar title-left/symbol-right composition.
- Communication effectiveness: 7/10 — Opportunities and Directory read clearly; collection subject is subordinate to slogan.

Average: 6.9/10.

WHAT WORKS:
1. The product/editorial distinction: quiet indexes, expressive destinations.
2. Shared Save, calendar, sorting and card conventions make movement between pages predictable.
3. The Women & Non-Binary cover's serif headline and indigo palette create an appealing emotional register; the contest cover has a different typographic energy.

WHAT DOESN'T, ranked:
1. Repeated unrelated card images and duplicate profiles weaken trust. These require asset/data decisions, not more styling.
2. The orbit looks like an atom. The relationship to this collection is not self-evident.
3. Twelve colors and four symbols are variation within one layout, not twelve fully developed editorial concepts.
4. Directory's generic summary fallback fills space without helping selection.

THE MOVE:
Develop the Women & Non-Binary cover as a typography-only “Take up space” composition. Remove the separate symbol and let type carry the idea. Prove that one cover before extending the principle to the other motifs.

## ◤ LOEWY — BEAUTY & TASTE REVIEW ◢

ONE-SECOND VERDICT
The indexes read as a calm, capable creative catalogue. The collections read as attractive campaign covers attached to that catalogue. The eye goes to the collection slogan first; category recognition follows too late on the mobile example.

SCORECARD
1. Instant legibility: 8/10
2. Simplicity / restraint: 7/10
3. Hierarchy & focus: 8/10
4. Honesty of form: 5/10
5. Proportion & rhythm: 7/10
6. Craft & finish: 7/10
7. MAYA calibration: 7/10
8. Emotional tone: 8/10
COMPOSITE: 7.1/10 · Weakest link: Honesty of form

THE TEARDOWN
The useful structure is already there. The white index is readable, familiar and composed. Directory's restored logos provide real identity at a modest scale. The collection typography and palettes give the catalogue warmth and ambition without disrupting the cards. Those are improvements worth protecting.

The weakest layer is applied decoration. An atom-shaped orbit beside an invitation to take up space is not yet an honest expression of the subject. Repeated landscape photos occupy substantial card area without distinguishing the opportunities below them. The shell suggests careful curation while the content sometimes suggests automated assembly.

The balance between fresh and familiar is broadly right: approachable controls with bolder editorial introductions. Push the conceptual specificity, not the number of flourishes. The likely audience aspiration is to feel recognized and capable of finding a relevant next step; the likely anxiety is wasting time on generic or unreliable listings. These are design hypotheses, not findings from user interviews.

THE 7 MOVES (ranked)
1. Remove the orbital symbol from the Women & Non-Binary cover — honesty of form — lets the strong type express the idea.
2. Remove generic Directory fallback sentences — simplification — reduces reading without losing information.
3. Reduce the image area or use an identity treatment when only a repeated fallback is available — function — makes card space earn its size.
4. Remove unnecessary mobile cover gaps and the detached motif column — proportion — brings results into view sooner.
5. Keep the actual collection name legible at 13–14px — instant legibility — improves orientation without sacrificing the headline.
6. Retain one shared card and control system across all palettes — MAYA — preserves confidence as the mood changes.
7. Verify and resolve duplicate identities upstream — honesty — makes the directory's polished promise credible.

VERDICT: REFINE — the structure and tone are good; conceptual specificity and content integrity limit the finish.
THE ONE THING: Replace interchangeable decorative imagery with type or organization-specific identity that communicates the actual subject.
DON'T TOUCH: White browse canvas, shared card actions, logo restoration, restrained controls, and the expressive serif/sans contrast on collection covers.

## Synthesis and priority

All three lenses agree on specificity over additional decoration. Casey is hardest on metaphor; Creative Direction is most receptive to the new cover energy; Loewy places the strongest emphasis on subtraction and practical browsing.

Recommended order:
1. One typography-led collection cover experiment, preserving its palette and headline.
2. A deliberate opportunity-image fallback policy, followed by Directory identity cleanup as a separately scoped data task.
3. Mobile cover density and redundant Directory copy.

These are recommendations, not changes applied during this review. They do not invalidate the previously passed technical checks, and those technical checks do not prove editorial quality.
