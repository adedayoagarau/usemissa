## Decision this study must make

Does reusable private preparation reduce application work? Does transfer assistance improve on that enough to justify installation, permissions and ongoing adapter maintenance?

No workspace redesign or universal bridge claim follows from local automation alone. See `application-bridge-evidence-review-2026-09-05.md` for completed experiments and limitations.

## Three conditions

| Condition | Experience | What it tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: Current workflow | Creator's normal tools, including destination-native saved information and materials | Honest baseline; do not force unnecessary retyping |
| B: Prepared materials | Organized synthetic bio, letter, work title and file, with ordinary copy/download | Value of private preparation without direct fill |
| C: Assisted transfer | Same materials plus a supported, reviewed field transfer | Incremental value beyond preparation |

Do not run C on an unsupported editor. Record unsupported rather than substituting a cooperative fixture and calling it the same platform. On phones, use B unless a real supported C mechanism exists. Record C as unavailable, not zero seconds or a failure by the participant.

## Participants and order

Start with six consenting creators as a formative study, including writing, visual work and interdisciplinary practice. This is not a statistically powered conversion experiment. Include iPhone Safari, Android Chrome and desktop users; record OS/browser versions and extension familiarity.

Use equivalent task variants and distribute the six orders ABC, ACB, BAC, BCA, CAB, CBA where all conditions are available. For two-condition phone sessions, alternate AB/BA. Do not ask participants to repeat exactly the same prose; memory would bias later attempts. Keep requirements and document size comparable across variants.

If only one participant/device is available, run a feasibility session and explicitly report that boundary. Do not extrapolate to all creators.

## Before a session

- Record test-call URL, organizer authorization, whether drafts are visible, and which actions are authorized. No live selection material or paid entries.
- Use synthetic materials with no private addresses, credentials, unpublished manuscripts or copyrighted excerpts.
- Obtain agreement to the study. Screen recording is optional and needs participant consent; otherwise an observer records actions and timing. Do not record sign-in secrets.
- Keep a clean draft for each condition. Use normal authentication and destination-native reuse; record preparation and installation time separately.
- Confirm clear labeling of test submissions and organizer cleanup arrangements before any external submission. If only draft testing is authorized, stop at draft recovery.

## Participant task script

“Using the supplied fictional creator materials, prepare an application for this test call. Choose the requested work, add its title and cover letter, attach the file, and check the requirements. Save your draft, leave the page, reopen it and verify that the content and attachment remain correct. Stop before final submission unless the facilitator explicitly identifies this as an authorized test submission.”

Do not tell participants where to click unless they are stuck; record every intervention. Ask afterward: “What felt repetitive?”, “What did you expect to be saved?”, “Where were you unsure?”, and “Would you use this again without help?”

## Measurement

Use a monotonic stopwatch for active task time. Record pauses and external delays separately. Do not replace observations with assumed seconds per click.

- First-use setup: account/material import, installation and permissions.
- Active task: finding materials through verified draft reopen.
- Counts: retyped fields, copy/pastes, tab/app switches, downloads, uploads, corrections and facilitator interventions.
- Integrity: exact text/line breaks; intended file/version; no overwritten destination answers; declarations and fees not selected automatically.
- Recovery: saved text and actual uploaded file survive reopening; unsaved/unuploaded states are clear.
- Completion: distinguish prepared, draft saved, creator says submitted and provider receipt. Missing receipt remains unknown.
- Preference: participant choice with reason; abandonment or opting out is a result, not discarded data.

A successful HTTP response or visible text does not prove draft persistence. Compare against the reopened destination and, where authorized, receipt ID. Keep only synthetic content and redacted evidence in the study log.

## Decision rules established before sessions

- Any silent text loss, wrong file, overwritten answer, unintended declaration or submission blocks that adapter until fixed and retested.
- Compare B against A first. If preparation adds work, simplify or stop that path.
- Compare C against B next, including first-use setup and review costs. If its advantage disappears, keep B and defer the extension.
- Report each participant/device separately, then median and range for comparable completed tasks. Include failures and missing conditions. Do not announce a percentage saving from an unrepresentative handful of sessions.
- A positive formative result permits a limited pilot only; sustained completion and maintenance cost require longer observation.
