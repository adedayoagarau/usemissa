# Missa Willingness-to-Pay Interview Guide

**Status:** ready to run. No code dependency.
**Date:** 2026-09-30
**Reads with:** `missa-pricing-entitlement-mapping.md`

## Goal

Turn the pricing hypothesis into observed evidence. The interviews answer the
unknowns the research could not: what a repeat applicant or a community
publisher actually values enough to pay for, and at what price the offer
becomes exploitative or irrelevant. They are not a sales exercise; every price
below the headline remains a hypothesis until a live pilot confirms it.

## Sampling

Recruit 12–18 people total, deliberately spread so no single cell dominates:

- Opportunity goal: grant, residency, fellowship, scholarship, contest, open call.
- Application frequency: first-time, occasional, repeat (3+ active at once).
- Income stability: student, emerging, established independent, institutional employee.
- Geography: at least one-third Nigeria and other non-USD, weak-currency markets.
- Current method: spreadsheet, Duotrope, Chill Subs, email, nothing.

Do not treat B2C versus B2B as the only split; a small mission-driven
organization is often as price-sensitive as a creator.

## Core questions (creator side)

1. Walk me through the last opportunity you applied to. What did you use to
   remember it, prepare it, and confirm you sent it?
2. What is the single most expensive part of that process in your time?
3. If a tool removed that step, how much would you pay per month? Start by
   naming a number, then ask what would make it "too much" and "a bargain".
4. Would a non-renewing 30- or 90-day pass be easier to commit to than a monthly
   auto-renewing subscription? Why?
5. Which of these would you pay for, and which feel like they should be free:
   discovery, official links, reminders, reusable material, comparison, history?
6. Does a paid tier make you trust the product more or less? Why?

## Core questions (organization side)

1. Describe your last call cycle end to end. Where did it break or take the
   most staff time?
2. Do you run calls year-round or seasonally? What happens to your tools during
   a closed season?
3. Would you prefer a flat annual price or a per-cycle / usage-based price?
   What makes a fixed bill feel punitive?
4. How many active calls and reviewer seats do you actually need this cycle?
5. Who in your organization would approve the purchase, and what would they
   need to see first?

## Structured probes

- Anchoring: always capture "too much" and "too little" around any number the
  respondent names, not just the point estimate.
- Paywall reaction: ask directly whether a creator tier that gates workflow
  (not access) feels fair, and whether a free tier would remain believable.
- Payment rails: for Nigeria respondents, ask whether card auto-renew is
  available to them, and whether a one-time pass is preferable.

## What to record

Per respondent: segment cell, current method, named price range (floor, point,
ceiling), pass-versus-subscription preference, fair-free boundary, and the one
feature they would pay for first. Store these as a structured sheet, not prose,
so the pilot can reuse the exact value claims.

## Exit criteria

Stop interviewing when the last three respondents add no new value metric or
price fence. The output is a short synthesis: the one creator value metric, the
one organization value metric, the provisional creator price band, and the
payment-rail constraint, each tagged with confidence.
