# Trends shaping how writers and artists find and submit to open calls, 2022–2026

Research date: 2026-10-09. Labels: **[DATA]** is a measured figure, **[REPORTED]** is a journalistic account of events, **[OPINION]** is commentary or advocacy, **[COMPANY]** is a self-reported company figure. Where I saw a figure only in a search summary and did not open the page, it is marked "(snippet)". Check those before you quote them in a deck.

## 1. Reading fees and entry fees

### Takeaway
Most literary magazines still read for free. Fees cluster at the top: about 13% of all magazines Chill Subs tracks charge, against 56% of the 100 most popular. Writers nonetheless submit to fee-charging magazines at nearly twice the rate they submit to free ones. A clear fee field, plus a fee-free filter, is a real decision aid, and writers outside the US feel the cost hardest.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, May 7, 2025]** Chill Subs data, published by its cofounder Benjamin Davis: about 13% of all tracked magazines charge fees, 24% of the top 1,000 and 56% of the top 100. Davis calls this "not a slippery slope. It's a cliff." — [Literary Hub, "The Rise of the Submission Industrial Complex"](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[DATA, May 2025]** Writers are "nearly twice as likely" to submit to fee-based magazines as to free ones (Chill Subs platform data). A typical published piece draws about 20 submissions. — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[DATA, May 2025]** A $3 fee leaves a magazine about $1.86 before platform fees. The Sun charges $2.50 and pays Submittable about $18,000 a year plus transaction fees. After The Sun added its fee, its paid submissions fell from about 36,000 to about 24,000 a year. — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[DATA, May 2025]** New literary magazines averaged about 40 launches a year from 2000 to 2015. Launches then reached about 200 in 2020, 201 in 2021 and 194 in 2022, roughly a fivefold rise. The growing field makes discovery harder. — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[OPINION, May 2025]** Equity: writer Karan Kapoor says a $3–5 fee can equal a meal or a day's wages in India, and that 100 submissions would cost more than a month's salary for many young professionals there. Davis argues that fees filter "Not for quality, but for who can afford to participate," and calls free options that make writers jump through hoops "a performative gesture." — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[REPORTED, May 2025]** Fee-free models and waivers:
  - ONLY POEMS keeps free reading windows and pays its contributors.
  - The Sun offers no-questions-asked fee waivers.
  - About 10% of Chill Subs members use its no-explanation scholarships.
  - A compiled list of African magazines found that none charge submission fees.
  — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[OPINION, date not confirmed]** SFWA ran a piece titled "The Egregious Practice of Charging Reading Fees." SFWA's long-standing position is that markets should not charge writers. — [SFWA](https://www.sfwa.org/?p=37918)
- **[OPINION, Sept 11, 2025]** Authors Publish covers the ongoing practice of charging writers to submit (snippet). — [Authors Publish](https://authorspublish.com/the-ever-evolving-game-of-charging-writers-for-submissions/)
- **[OPINION, 2023]** Neil Clarke said in 2023 that "pay-to-submit sacrifices too many [legitimate] authors" and rejected fees as a defence against AI spam (snippet via secondary coverage). — [MetaStellar](https://metastellar.com/nonfiction/news/clarkesworld-swamped-by-ai-spam-temporary-stops-accepting-submissions)
- Fee amounts: general reading fees typically run $2–5 (average about $3) and contest fees about $10–15 (snippets, undated commentary). — [NewPages](https://newpages.com/blog/blog-items/literary-magazine-submissions-how-much-will-you-pay); [Novlr](https://www.novlr.org/the-reading-room/should-writers-pay-submission-fees-everything-you-need-to-know)

### Inferences
- Fees are concentrated at the magazines writers most want. That makes a visible, accurate fee field (amount, waiver available, free window) high-value. It also suggests a "free to enter" filter and a "waivers available" flag.
- Clarkesworld's AI spam (section 2) makes some editors more likely to add fees, and others refuse. The fee landscape will likely stay mixed and keep changing, which favours a source that keeps fee data current.

### Gaps
- I found no formal, scientific 2022–2026 survey of writer sentiment on fees. Brevity ran an informal reader poll (about 300 responses), but I could not confirm its date.
- I found no Poets & Writers or Electric Literature data piece specifically on fee prevalence.
- I did not check Chill Subs' original report for its method.

## 2. AI-generated submission floods and artists' concerns about AI training

### Takeaway
In February 2023, Clarkesworld closed submissions after 500 machine-written stories arrived against 700 genuine ones. Through 2024 it stayed open by improving its own filtering, which Clarke called "a band aid, not a cure." AI detectors are widely judged unreliable. Writer surveys show near-unanimous demand for consent before AI trains on their work. This directly supports Missa's "nothing trains on your work" promise.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA/REPORTED, Feb 2023]** Clarkesworld closed submissions on Feb 20, 2023. Neil Clarke said that by that point the magazine had received 700 legitimate and 500 machine-written submissions that month. — [NPR, Feb 24, 2023](https://www.npr.org/2023/02/24/1159286436/ai-chatbot-chatgpt-magazine-clarkesworld-artificial-intelligence); [NPR, Feb 23, 2023](https://www.npr.org/2023/02/23/1159118948/sci-fi-magazine-stops-submissions-after-flood-of-ai-generated-stories)
- **[DATA, Feb 2023]** The volume grew fast. About 50 suspected AI stories arrived in December 2022, roughly double that in January 2023, and more than 500 by Feb 20, 2023. At that pace AI submissions were heading towards a 1:1 ratio with genuine ones. — [Marketplace](https://marketplace.org/episode/what-happens-when-robots-write-sci-fi)
- **[REPORTED, 2023]** Submissions stayed closed from Feb 20 to March 12, 2023. It was the first indefinite closure in the magazine's 17-year history. — [BuzzFeed News](https://www.buzzfeednews.com/pranavdixit/clarkesworld-sci-fi-magazine-ai-story-submissions-closed)
- **[DATA, early 2024]** Clarkesworld's "2023 Submissions Snapshot" says software changes let it avoid closing again "despite continued surges." Clarke called this "a band aid, not a cure" and said higher volumes could force future closures (snippet). — [Clarkesworld 2023 Submissions Snapshot (Goodreads mirror)](https://www.goodreads.com/author_blog_posts/24472523-2023-clarkesworld-submissions-snapshot?tab=book)
- **[OPINION, 2023]** Clarke said detection tools proved "unreliable against our test data," and that "none of the anti-GPT stuff is any good… easily outwitted." He also said third-party verification tools are expensive and don't work everywhere. — [Quartz Q&A](https://qz.com/clarkesword-neil-clarke-chatgpt-ai-q-and-a-1850144881); [MetaStellar](https://metastellar.com/nonfiction/news/clarkesworld-swamped-by-ai-spam-temporary-stops-accepting-submissions)
- **[REPORTED, Poets & Writers, date not confirmed, about 2024]** "Managing Submissions in the Age of AI" reports:
  - Clarkesworld bans any work touched by AI and calls such work "the fruit of a poisoned tree."
  - Autumn House Press changed its guidelines to forbid AI-generated work after a contest incident.
  - CLMP's Mary Gannon advises magazines to state an explicit AI policy, require disclosure or attestation, and possibly run checks with Copyleaks or GPTZero.
  — [Poets & Writers](https://www.pw.org/content/managing_submissions_in_the_age_of_ai)
- **[REPORTED, Jan 2024]** Rie Qudan, winner of the Akutagawa Prize, said about 5% of her winning book used verbatim ChatGPT text, which prompted debate over AI in prizes (snippet). — [Poets & Writers](https://pw.org/node/1170676)
- **[REPORTED, July 2025]** Grant funders face the same problem. NIH notice NOT-OD-25-132, issued July 17, 2025, limits applications substantially developed by AI and caps how many applications each principal investigator can submit a year. It applies to due dates from Sept 25, 2025 (snippet via secondary source). — [CASRAI](https://casrai.org/news/ai-grant-application-checklist)
- **[DATA, Dec 2023]** In an Authors Guild survey of more than 2,400 authors, 96% said consent should be required and authors paid when their work trains AI. Only 3% said use without consent or payment was acceptable. — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/ag-ai-survey-reveals-authors-overwhelmingly-want-consent-and-compensation-for-use-of-their-works/)
- **[DATA, Jan–Apr 2024]** The UK Society of Authors surveyed members in January 2024 (about 800 responses). 26% of illustrators and 36% of translators had already lost work to generative AI. Respondents showed "almost unanimous" consensus for regulation based on transparency and consent. — [International Authors Forum](https://internationalauthors.org/news/society-of-authors-ai-survey-results-published/); [Books+Publishing, Apr 15, 2024](https://www.booksandpublishing.com.au/articles/2024/04/15/250317/a-third-of-translators-report-losing-work-due-to-ai/)
- **[DATA, 2025]** In an Australian Society of Authors survey, 98% said AI companies should ask permission before training on authors' and illustrators' work (snippet). — [ASA](https://www.asauthors.org.au/?p=21536)
- **[DATA, UK, around 2024–25]** ALCS authors' support for AI training depends on conditions: 91% want permission, 96% want fair pay and 87% want credit (snippet). — [Publishers Weekly](https://publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/digital/copyright/article/96647-u-k-authors-weigh-in-on-ai-licensing-in-new-survey.html)

### Inferences
- Organisations are now raising the cost of submitting (AI attestations, submission caps, bans). Writers therefore gain from a tool that shows each call's AI policy and eligibility rules clearly before they apply.
- Consent figures of 96–98% suggest a "nothing trains on your work" promise speaks to a near-universal concern, not a niche one. It works as trust positioning, especially next to tools that do process uploaded manuscripts.
- Missa never handles the work, so it sits outside the AI-detection arms race. It can help organisations reach genuine applicants and help artists find calls, without taking any part in screening.

### Gaps
- I found no 2025–2026 Clarkesworld or Neil Clarke figures on AI submission volume. His blog (neil-clarke.com) should be checked directly.
- I found no Verge, NYT or Guardian piece with newer magazine-wide data, and no current list of contests that ban AI.

## 3. Growth or decline: magazines, residencies and arts funding, 2025–2026

### Takeaway
The supply of US literary venues is under financial stress. In May 2025 the NEA terminated or withdrew grants to many, possibly all, of its 51 Literary Arts grantees. Congress then rejected the NEA's elimination for FY2026 and held its funding at $207M, but the FY2027 fight is under way. In the UK, Creative Scotland closed its Open Fund for Individuals in 2024 because money was short, and remaining funds are heavily oversubscribed. Calls are becoming more volatile: they open, close, change and disappear. That raises the value of a source that tracks them closely.

### Cited Findings
- **[REPORTED, May 2025]** The count of affected grantees varies by date and source:
  - Publishers Lunch: 40 of 51 had been terminated or withdrawn at the time of reporting.
  - Publishers Weekly: at least 38 of the 51 CLMP member publishers with 2025 NEA grants, together worth over $1.2M.
  - Mary Gannon of CLMP, later: "All 51 of this year's grantees in the Literary Arts category received either termination letters or withdrawal letters."
  — [Publishers Lunch](https://lunch.publishersmarketplace.com/2025/05/nea-begins-terminating-and-withdrawing-grants/); [Publishers Weekly, May 12, 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250512/97702-nea-literary-grants-staff-cut-as-trump-proposes-eliminating-the-agency.html); [Los Angeles Review of Books](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/we-dont-have-any-reserves/)
- **[DATA, May 2025]** Reported losses by grantee:

  | Grantee | Amount |
  | --- | --- |
  | Paris Review | $15,000 |
  | McSweeney's | $25,000 |
  | One Story | $20,000 (already received and in use) |
  | ZYZZYVA | $12,500 |
  | n+1 | $12,500 |
  | Transit Books | $40,000 |
  | Milkweed Editions | $50,000 |
  | Arte Público Press | $40,000 |

  — [Authors Guild](https://authorsguild.org/news/ag-condemns-nea-grant-terminations-and-devastating-impacts/)
- **[REPORTED, May 2025]** Electric Literature was also on the list of terminated grantees. — [Publishers Weekly](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250512/97702-nea-literary-grants-staff-cut-as-trump-proposes-eliminating-the-agency.html)
- **[REPORTED, May 2025]** The termination letters cited new priorities "as prioritized by the President." The administration's FY2026 budget proposed eliminating the NEA, NEH and IMLS. — [NPR, May 3, 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/05/03/nx-s1-5385888/); [American Theatre, May 2, 2025](https://americantheatre.org/2025/05/02/trump-proposes-elimination-of-nea-and-neh)
- **[REPORTED, FY2026]** The NEA dropped its Challenge America grant program for FY2026 and shifted priorities towards America's 250th anniversary (snippet). — [Legis1](https://legis1.com/news/congress-blocks-trump-arts-agencies-blocked)
- **[REPORTED, FY2026–27]** Congress passed P.L. 119-74 and 119-75, keeping NEA grant funding at $207M, the same as FY2025. The House Appropriations Committee has since approved FY2027 cuts to the NEA and NEH. I did not verify the final FY2027 outcome (snippet). — [Legis1](https://legis1.com/news/congress-blocks-trump-arts-agencies-blocked); [Hyperallergic](https://hyperallergic.com/house-proposes-slashing-major-arts-education-grant.md)
- **[REPORTED, 2024]** Creative Scotland closed its Open Fund for Individuals over a £6.6M funding gap that the Scottish Government had not confirmed. Funding decisions in October 2024 were described as "hugely oversubscribed." — [Irish News / PA](https://www.irishnews.com/entertainment/creative-scotlands-hands-tied-when-key-fund-was-closed-chief-executive-says-TWPFLOEIB5K2TFZ7B45BENUBQI/)
- **[DATA, 2025]** Creative Capital's 2025 Awards drew 5,653 applications for $2.45M in grants to 55 artists, a selection rate of about 1%. — [Creative Capital press release](https://creative-capital.org/press/announcing-the-2025-creative-capital-awards)
- **[DATA, undated]** Residency counts: about 500 artists' communities in the US and more than 1,500 worldwide, serving about 30,000 residents a year. This comes from a third-party summary of Artist Communities Alliance figures, and I could not confirm the original source or year. — [Neighbourhood Arts Network](https://neighbourhoodartsnetwork.org/learning-room/space/international-residencies-list)
- **[DATA, May 2025]** Literary magazine launches stayed near 200 a year from 2020 to 2022 (Chill Subs). — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)

### Inferences
- Two forces pull in opposite directions. Many new small magazines keep launching while established nonprofit venues lose public money. The result is churn: more venues, less stable ones, and fees as a possible response to lost grants. A directory that tracks status (open, closed, paused, fee changed) has more value in a high-churn market.
- Funder applications are extremely competitive (about 1% at Creative Capital), so artists need to apply widely. That increases the deadline load (section 6).

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative data on residency growth from 2022 to 2026. The Artist Communities Alliance research page (https://artistcommunities.org/research) should be checked.
- I found no count of literary magazine closures from 2023 to 2026.
- I did not verify Arts Council England's 2025–26 figures or the final FY2027 NEA outcome.

## 4. Creator economy and willingness to pay for creative tools

### Takeaway
Paid subscriptions from creators to their audiences are growing fast; Substack passed 5 million paid subscriptions in March 2025. Creators say they want direct, platform-independent channels. I found no direct data on how many writers or artists pay for submission or discovery tools, so willingness to pay for a tool like Missa is unproven by public data.

### Cited Findings
- **[COMPANY, Mar 2025]** Substack passed 5 million paid subscriptions about four months after passing 4 million (November 2024). It has more than 50,000 paid publications. — [Tubefilter, Mar 12, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/12/substack-five-million-paid-subscribers-journalist-reporter-newsletter/)
- **[COMPANY/ESTIMATE]** Paid subscriptions rose from about 2M (2023) to 4M (November 2024) to 5M (March 2025). Total active subscriptions, paid and free, exceed 35M. Paid subscriptions are not the same as unique paying people. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/12/substack-five-million-paid-subscribers-journalist-reporter-newsletter/); [Wikipedia: Substack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substack)
- **[REPORTED, 2025]** Substack later raised $100M at a $1.1B valuation, as reported by the NYT (snippet). — [Wikipedia: Substack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substack)
- **[COMPANY SURVEY, Feb 2025]** Patreon's first "State of Create" report (1,000 US creators and 2,000 fans, fielded by NewtonX):
  - 78% of creators say algorithms affect what they make.
  - 53% say it is harder to reach followers than five years ago.
  - 70% report unpredictable monthly income.
  - 75% say platforms punish creators who don't post constantly.
  - 78% say burnout affects their motivation.
  - 81% want a direct channel to their fans.
  — [Tubefilter, Feb 19, 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/19/patreon-state-of-create-report-fans-followers/); [NetInfluencer](https://www.netinfluencer.com/patreon-2025-state-of-create-report-how-creators-are-breaking-free-from-platform-control/)
- **[DATA, 2024–25]** Artists' incomes are low, which limits what they can pay:
  - UK (Creative United and Coventry University, 271 visual artists, 2024 earnings, published November 2025): 56% earned under £5,000 from art sales; median artistic income was £12,500, against a £24,000 Living Wage benchmark.
  - Kingston, Canada (2024 data): 50% earned under $4,999 from artistic work.
  — [Creative United](https://www.creativeunited.org.uk/services/understanding-uk-artists-livelihoods-the-2024-survey/); [Arts Kingston](https://www.artskingston.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Artist-Prosperity-Project-Anonymized-Data.pdf)
- **[DATA, Oct 2025]** The NORC National Survey of Artists (more than 2,600 US artists, fielded late 2024) found that 34% are self-employed and 11% juggle three or more jobs (snippet via press). — [ICPSR/University of Michigan](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/posts/shared/national-survey-of-artists-reveals-full-extent-of-americas-creative-workforce-and-their-economic-insecurity); [Mellon](https://www.mellon.org/news/new-national-study-offers-fresh-insight-lives-livelihoods-us-artists)

### Inferences
- Paying for subscriptions is now normal in writing culture through Substack. Creators, though, mostly pay as fans. Low artist incomes (median under £12.5k in the UK) suggest a low price point, or a free core with paid extras, will convert better.
- Chill Subs' scholarship uptake (about 10%, section 1) shows that a free or sponsored tier for low-income and international users is an established pattern in this market.

### Gaps
- I found no public data on the share of writers or artists who pay for submission or opportunity tools (Duotrope, Chill Subs Pro, Submittable) or on how much they spend. Duotrope's subscriber count and Chill Subs' paid conversion are not public in what I found.

## 5. Discovery shift: AI search, newsletters, social and aggregators

### Takeaway
Search is fragmenting. Google AI Overviews have raised zero-click rates, while ChatGPT referrals grow fast from a small base. Book discovery for readers runs heavily through TikTok. Both trends favour structured, citable, up-to-date listings that AI assistants and newsletters can point to.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, July 2025]** Similarweb figures:
  - ChatGPT referrals to news sites rose from under 1M (Jan–May 2024) to more than 25M (Jan–May 2025).
  - News searches ending without a click rose from 56% to about 69% between May 2024 (launch of AI Overviews) and May 2025.
  - Organic search visits to news sites fell from more than 2.3B (mid-2024) to under 1.7B.
  — [TechCrunch, July 2, 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/02/chatgpt-referrals-to-news-sites-are-growing-but-not-enough-to-offset-search-declines)
- **[DATA, 2025]** In June 2025, ChatGPT sent 396.8M referral visits to the 1,000 sites Similarweb tracks, 81.7% of all AI referral traffic to those sites. — [HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/chatgpt-now-accounts-for-80percent-of-ai-traffic-to-top-websites)
- **[DATA, 2025, secondary]** Pew found users clicked a link 8% of the time when an AI Overview appeared, against 15% without one (snippet via secondary source). — [PPC Land](https://ppc.land/chatgpt-referrals-surge-as-google-searches-hit-zero-click-records/)
- **[DATA, Jan 2025]** Circana BookScan: sales by BookTok authors grew nearly 20% in 2024, the fifth straight year of growth, while overall print units grew less than 1%. — [Jane Friedman](https://janefriedman.com/circana-bookscan-releases-a-brief-2024-sales-analysis/); [Publishers Weekly](https://www2.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250113/96842-print-book-sales-saw-a-small-sales-increase-in-2024.html)
- **[COMPANY SURVEY, Feb 2025]** Fans spend only 24% of their Instagram time, 32% of YouTube time and 38% of TikTok time on creators they follow. Social reach is algorithmic, not based on followers. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/19/patreon-state-of-create-report-fans-followers/)
- **[REPORTED]** Opportunity aggregators already exist: Chill Subs, Submittable, Duotrope, ArtConnect, and newsletters such as Lit Mag News on Substack. Creative Capital's call is relisted across aggregators (ArtConnect, Grantable, regional arts councils). — [ArtConnect listing](https://www.artconnect.com/opportunity/h4x_k4p36e9V1Fdl6KESB); [Lit Mag News](https://litmagnews.substack.com/p/q-how-do-we-spot-scammy-lit-mags-b40)

### Inferences
- When an AI assistant summarises "open calls for poets this month," it needs current, structured sources. A directory with clean deadline, fee and eligibility fields, and pages that are easy to cite, is well placed to be cited. Assistants are likely to return stale or wrong deadlines without such a source.
- BookTok drives readers to books, not writers to calls. Its relevance to Missa is as a distribution channel for reaching writers, not as a competing way to discover calls.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how writers or artists specifically find open calls (newsletter compared with social, search or word of mouth). This is a key gap and would be worth a primary survey of Missa users.
- I found no data on AI-assistant traffic to opportunity directories.

## 6. Deadline and admin burden

### Takeaway
Artists juggle several jobs and very low acceptance rates, and many do unpaid work hoping for opportunities. I found no recent survey that measures hours spent on applications. That makes the admin-burden claim plausible but not quantified, and it should be presented as inference.

### Cited Findings
- **[DATA, 2024 data, published 2026]** Kingston, Canada: 29% of artists frequently and 48% occasionally do unpaid work for exposure or future opportunities. Of those, only 8% said it improved their finances and 38% said it did not. — [Arts Kingston Artist Prosperity Project](https://www.artskingston.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Artist-Prosperity-Project-Anonymized-Data.pdf)
- **[DATA, Oct 2025]** NORC National Survey of Artists: 11% hold three or more jobs (snippet). — [ICPSR](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/posts/shared/national-survey-of-artists-reveals-full-extent-of-americas-creative-workforce-and-their-economic-insecurity)
- **[DATA, 2025]** Creative Capital: 5,653 applications for 55 awards. Submitting literary work takes about 20 submissions per published piece. Both figures imply each artist makes many applications. — [Creative Capital](https://creative-capital.org/press/announcing-the-2025-creative-capital-awards); [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[DATA, Oct 2024]** Theatre Network Australia's "This Is How We Do It 2024" (273 independent performing artists) includes a section, "The Other Work," on non-art labour. I did not see figures for admin hours (snippet). — [TNA PDF](https://tna.org.au/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/2024-THIS-IS-HOW-WE-DO-IT_FINAL.pdf)
- **[DATA, Feb 2025]** 75% of creators say platforms punish those who don't publish constantly, and 78% report burnout. This relates to burnout among creators generally, not to applications. — [Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/19/patreon-state-of-create-report-fans-followers/)

### Inferences
- Applying is a volume game: about 1% success at Creative Capital and about 20 submissions per acceptance for writers. That turns deadline tracking into a real organising task, which supports reminders and saved lists as the core value.

### Gaps
- I found no 2022–2026 survey from Creative Capital, a-n, Artists' Union England or Americans for the Arts that quantifies time spent on applications or admin. The full CREATe 2024 report (https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/342634) and the TNA 2024 report may contain relevant figures. a-n is refreshing its Artists Payment Guide but has not published a livelihoods survey that I could find for 2024–25.

## 7. Equity and access: fee waivers, international artists, Global South

### Takeaway
Fees and US-centric eligibility fall hardest on writers outside the US and on low-income writers. Waivers and scholarships are the accepted remedy but are uneven and often take effort to obtain. Many non-US scenes (for example African magazines) are already fee-free.

### Cited Findings
- **[OPINION/DATA, May 2025]** A $3–5 fee can equal a day's wages in India, and 100 submissions would cost more than a month's salary for many young professionals there. — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[DATA, May 2025]** About 10% of Chill Subs members use no-questions scholarships. A compiled list of African magazines found that none charge fees. The Sun offers no-questions waivers. — [Literary Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **[DATA, 2025]** Creative Capital's eligibility rules require applicants to be at least 25, to have five or more years of professional practice, and to be based in the US or its territories. US-only rules like these are common. — [Creative Capital Awards](https://creative-capital.org/creative-capital-awards)
- **[DATA, Nov 2025]** UK lower earning brackets are dominated by women and non-binary artists. About one in five respondents are disabled or have a long-term health condition. — [Creative United](https://www.creativeunited.org.uk/services/understanding-uk-artists-livelihoods-the-2024-survey/)
- **[OPINION, 2023]** Clarke said payment and verification tools "don't work everywhere," excluding some international writers. — [MetaStellar](https://metastellar.com/nonfiction/news/clarkesworld-swamped-by-ai-spam-temporary-stops-accepting-submissions)

### Inferences
- Filters for "open to international applicants," "fee waiver available" and "no fee" serve a real equity need. They also widen Missa's market beyond the US.

### Gaps
