# Supply side of the open-call market: organizations that publish calls, and the money behind them

Research date: 2026-10-09. Many figures come from directory and vendor marketing pages with no visible date. In those cases the "as of" is the date I accessed the page (Oct 2026), and the note says the page itself is undated. Directory counts overlap heavily, so do not add them together.

## 1. How many literary magazines and small presses exist?

### Takeaway
The best single number is roughly 7,800 currently active publishers and agents listed on Duotrope, a curated directory that covers fiction, nonfiction, poetry and visual art. Chill Subs reports a similar 5,000 to 7,500 magazines, contests and presses. Curated lists such as Poets & Writers run much smaller, about 670 to 1,000 magazines and about 240 presses. Over 22,000 more Duotrope listings are closed or defunct, which shows how much churn there is in the segment. New magazine launches rose about fivefold after 2020.

### Cited Findings
- **Duotrope's main listings page:** 7,775 "current" publishers and agents. Duotrope counts as current only those still publishing or accepting submissions. By category: Fiction 5,063, Nonfiction 5,090, Poetry 3,787, Visual Art 1,488. Categories overlap. The page is undated (accessed Oct 2026; direct fetch returned 403, so these figures come from search snippets). — [Duotrope: Our Listings by the Numbers](https://duotrope.com/about/listings/)
- **Duotrope catalog page:** 7,808 current publishers and agents, plus 22,187 closed, believed defunct, removed or DNQ listings. A third Duotrope page says 7,797. All are undated. — [Duotrope catalog](https://duotrope.com/search/catalog.aspx); [Duotrope list](https://duotrope.com/search/list/)
- **Duotrope category pages:** 5,099 nonfiction and 3,801 poetry publishers and agents (undated). — [Duotrope nonfiction](https://duotrope.com/about/listings/nonfiction); [Duotrope poetry](https://duotrope.com/about/listings/poetry)
- **Chill Subs:**
  - Its blog boilerplate gives totals between 6,919 and 7,499 for "literary magazines, writing contests, indie book publishers, and more". One version reads "a database of 7,144 literary magazines, writing contests, indie book publishers…" (2025–2026 posts).
  - The homepage shows 5,176 literary magazines and 98,135+ registered writers (undated).
  - An older BetaList listing says "3000+ literary magazines, 1200+ contests" (dated, likely about 2021–22).
  - Sources: [Chill Subs homepage](https://www.chillsubs.com/); [Chill Subs blog: Best Lit Mag Awards 2025 by the numbers](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/best-lit-mag-awards-2025-by-the-numbers); [BetaList](https://betalist.com/startups/chill-subs)
- **Chill Subs data on new launches:** about 40 new lit mags a year on average from 2000 to 2015, then 200 in 2020, 201 in 2021 and 194 in 2022. Chill Subs also has 50,000+ writers on the platform. — [Literary Hub, "The Rise of the Submission Industrial Complex," Benjamin Davis, May 7, 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **Poets & Writers homepage:** 670+ literary magazines and 240+ small presses in its curated databases (snapshot about mid-2026). — [Poets & Writers](https://www.pw.org/)
- **Poets & Writers Literary Magazines guide:** describes a "carefully vetted database of over one thousand literary magazine and journals". This conflicts with the 670+ figure, probably because of different dates or definitions. — [P&W Literary Journals & Magazines](https://www.pw.org/content/literary_magazines)
- **Poets & Writers open reading periods:** "over 200 literary magazines and small presses" were open to submissions at the time of the snapshot. — [P&W Open Reading Periods](https://www.pw.org/open-reading-periods)
- **Older P&W figures (2020):** 514 small presses, of which 405 publish poetry, and 1,200 literary magazines, of which 225 publish poetry. — [Poetry Foundation / Harriet, Feb 2020](https://www.poetryfoundation.org/harriet-books/2020/02/the-new-normal-how-we-gave-up-the-small-press)
- **Other Poets & Writers database:** P&W also runs a small press database. — [Winning Writers summary](https://winningwriters.com/resources/small-press-database-at-poets-writers-1)

### Inferences
- **Practical universe of active English-language outlets:** about 5,000–8,000 literary magazines, presses and agents that take unsolicited submissions. Duotrope's roughly 7,800 includes agents and book publishers, so the magazine-only figure is likely 5,000–6,000. Estimate, triangulated from Duotrope and the Chill Subs homepage.
- **Churn is high:** there are about 22k defunct Duotrope listings against 7.8k live ones. An org-side tool will face constant arrival and departure of small, volunteer-run customers.
- **The curated "quality" tier is about 1,000 magazines** (Poets & Writers). This tier is the most likely to pay for software and to charge fees.

### Gaps
- I found no published CLMP membership count. Search results mention CLMP's directory but no numbers. Check clmp.org/members directly.
- I found no current NewPages listing count.
- I could not fetch Duotrope's page directly (HTTP 403), so the publisher/agent split and the share of contests are not verified.

## 2. How many artist residencies exist worldwide and in the US?

### Takeaway
Directories list about 1,400–1,700 residencies worldwide (TransArtists), and Res Artis has about 700 member organizations in 80–85 countries. The US-centric Artist Communities Alliance (formerly Alliance of Artists Communities) directory has 300+ organizations. These lists overlap. A global universe of roughly 1,500–2,000 programs is a reasonable estimate.

### Cited Findings
- **TransArtists (DutchCulture), own residencies page:** "more than 1700 residencies" (undated, accessed Oct 2026). — [TransArtists residencies](https://www.transartists.org/en/residencies)
- **DutchCulture's TransArtists page:** "over 1400 artistic residencies". Older and lower. — [DutchCulture TransArtists](https://dutchculture.nl/en/transartists)
- **Res Artis:** a network of "700 centres, organisations and individuals in over 80 countries" (homepage, undated). Wikipedia says 700+ members in 85+ countries, and an older Artist Trust page says 650+ in 70+ countries. — [Res Artis](https://resartis.org/); [Wikipedia: Res Artis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Res_Artis); [Artist Trust](https://artisttrust.org/resources/res-artis/)
- **Alliance of Artists Communities / Artist Communities Alliance:** directory of "over 300 international artist residency organizations" (Wikipedia, secondary source). — [Wikipedia: Alliance of Artists Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_of_Artists_Communities)

### Inferences
- **Estimated global universe:** about 1,500–2,000 residency programs, about 300–500 of them in the US. This estimate rests on the TransArtists high count plus the ACA US-heavy directory. It is not a census.
- **Fee-charging is common:** many residency applications use application fees through Submittable, SlideRoom or CaFE, so residencies are a natural org-side segment. This is a qualitative inference; I found no source that measures it.

### Gaps
- I found no authoritative US-only residency count. Res Artis and ACA member lists would need to be scraped.
- I found no data on the residency application fee market.

## 3. How many arts grants and funders, and how much money?

### Takeaway
US arts, culture and humanities giving reached $27.31B in 2025 (Giving USA 2026). Foundations give about $4B a year to the arts, about 6% of foundation dollars in 2023 and falling. Public funding is much smaller and under pressure:
- **NEA:** $207M in FY2026, with a request to cut it to $29M in FY2027.
- **State arts agencies:** $646.0M in FY2026, down 5.9%.
- **Canada Council:** C$284.4M to 5,305 recipients in 2024-25.
- **Arts Council England Project Grants:** 9,642 applications for 3,084 awards in 2023/24.

Most of this money flows to organizations, not to open calls for individuals.

### Cited Findings
- **Giving USA 2026 (2025 giving):**
  - Arts, culture and humanities: $27.31B, up 7.5%, or 4.7% after inflation.
  - Total US giving: $617.2B.
  - Sources: [Giving USA press release, June 2026](https://givingusa.org/giving-usa-charitable-giving-rose-to-617-20-billion-in-2025-surpassing-the-600-billion-mark-for-the-first-time/); [Movers & Makers, Jun 23 2026](https://moversmakers.org/2026/06/23/u-s-charitable-giving-tops-record-617b-in-2025/)
- **Giving USA 2025 (2024 giving):** arts, culture and humanities $25.13B (up 9.5%), out of a $592.50B total. — [Lilly Family School of Philanthropy, 2025](https://philanthropy.indianapolis.iu.edu/news-events/news/_news/2025/giving-usa-2025.html)
- **Candid Foundation 1000 (2023 data):**
  - About $4B to arts and culture, or 6% of foundation grant dollars, the lowest share recorded. The share was 9% around 2013 and 13% in 2010.
  - Grants of $500K+ made up 52% of arts dollars.
  - General operating support was 32%.
  - The top 25 funders gave 39% of arts dollars.
  - Source: [GIA/Candid, Foundation Grants to Arts and Culture 2023](https://reader.giarts.org/read/foundation-grants-to-arts-and-culture-2023)
- **Candid 2021 data:** $3.5B, or 7%. — [GIA/Candid 2021](https://reader.giarts.org/read/foundation-grants-to-arts-and-culture-202101)
- **NEA FY2026:**
  - Funded at $207M, including the 40% allocation to state and regional arts agencies.
  - The President's FY2027 request (April 3, 2026) proposes $29M to close the agency. Congress has not acted.
  - Sources: [NASAA, Feb 2026: NEA Funded for FY2026](https://nasaa-arts.org/newsletter/2026-nasaa-notes-issues/february-2026-nasaa-notes/nea-funded-for-fy2026/); [OpenGrants](https://opengrants.io/nea-grants-whats-open-now-despite-closure-push/)
- **NEA cancellations (2025):**
  - In May 2025 the NEA cancelled many existing grants and withdrew grant offers. One outlet reported that "over 50%" of open awards were terminated; this is a secondary report, not an official NEA figure.
  - The NEA cancelled the FY2026 Creative Writing Fellowships (Aug 2025).
  - Sources: [ARTnews](https://www.artnews.com/art-news/news/national-endowment-for-the-arts-cancels-grants-trump-1234740558/); [NPR, Aug 26 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/08/26/nx-s1-5518202/nea-cancels-creative-writing-fellowship); [NEA FY2026 update](https://www.arts.gov/news/press-releases/2025/updates-national-endowment-arts-fy-2026-grant-opportunities)
- **NASAA FY2026 (released Feb 2026):**
  - State arts agency legislative appropriations: $646.0M, down 5.9%.
  - Excluding line items: $572.9M, up 2.6%.
  - 25 agencies increased and 20 decreased.
  - Arts Midwest cites a FY2025 base of $694.3M, which implies a 6.9% drop. The FY2025 base may have been revised.
  - Sources: [NASAA FY2026 State Arts Agency Revenues (PDF via Wisconsin Arts Board)](https://artsboard.wisconsin.gov/Documents/FY2026-State-Arts-Agency-Revenues-Report_final-updated.pdf); [Arts Midwest 2026](https://artsmidwest.org/resources/research-data/public-funding-for-the-arts-in-the-midwest-2026-by-the-numbers/)
- **Canada Council 2024-25:**
  - C$284.4M in grants to 5,305 artists, groups and organizations.
  - Explore and Create stream data is published separately.
  - Sources: [Canada Council Funding](https://canadacouncil.ca/funding); [Arts Abroad 2024-25](https://canadacouncil.ca/research/research-library/2025/09/2024-25-arts-abroad-funding-results)
- **Arts Council England, 2023/24 National Lottery Project Grants:**
  - 9,642 applications (up 9.7% from 8,792), with 3,084 projects funded, a success rate of about 32%.
  - 1,204 of the awards (39%) went to individuals.
  - The 2024/25 award list is published as a spreadsheet. I did not find a published 2024/25 total.
  - Sources: [ACE Annual Report 2023/24](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67290789fbd69e1861921b18/Arts_Council_Annual_Report_and_Accounts_202324__1_.pdf); [ACE Project Grants data](https://www.artscouncil.org.uk/ProjectGrants/project-grants-data)

### Inferences
- **The individual-artist share is small.** Most philanthropic and public arts money goes to institutions through general operating support. Only a small fraction of the $27B goes out through competitive open calls to individuals. I found no reliable figure for that slice.
- **Government volumes give an anchor for application counts.** ACE Project Grants alone handle about 10k applications a year, and Canada Council makes 5,305 grants a year. Both are large funders with in-house systems. That points Missa's org-side opportunity toward the long tail of small funders, foundations and arts nonprofits rather than national councils.
- **Federal cuts may push application volume onto other funders.** The NEA cuts and proposed closure in 2025–26 may shift demand to state agencies, foundations and private open calls. This is speculative.

### Gaps
- I found no count of US foundations that make grants to individual artists. Candid's Foundation Directory has one, but it is paywalled.
- I found no ACE 2024/25 pound total for Project Grants.
- I found no NEA FY2026 number of grants awarded.

## 4. How many writing contests, film festivals, juried art calls and photo competitions?

### Takeaway
FilmFreeway says 12,000+ film festivals and screenplay contests use it, up from the 9,000+ figure in the brief, and 2M+ filmmakers. CaFE lists between about 1,300 and 5,700 visual art calls a year from about 850–1,200 organizations. In 2024, CaFE took 186,083 entries at a $20.22 average fee. Chill Subs once counted 1,200+ writing contests. Winning Writers tracks 200+ free contests. I found no authoritative count for photography competitions.

### Cited Findings
- **FilmFreeway:**
  - "Over 12,000 of the world's best film festivals and screenplay contests", including about 234 Academy/BAFTA-accredited festivals, reaching "over two million filmmakers" (homepage, undated, accessed Oct 2026).
  - More than 1 million entries submitted via FilmFreeway have become Official Selections (cumulative).
  - Sources: [FilmFreeway](https://filmfreeway.com/); [FilmFreeway How It Works](https://filmfreeway.com/pages/how-it-works)
- **Per-festival example:** Julien Dubuque International Film Festival receives 1,500+ submissions a year and selects about 120. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Dubuque_International_Film_Festival)
- **CaFE (Creative West, formerly WESTAF), 2024 recap:**
  - 186,083 entries submitted, at a $20.22 average entry fee.
  - Call mix: exhibitions 53.5%, public art 22.3%.
  - Source: [CaFE, Looking Back at 2024](https://www.callforentry.org/looking-back-at-2024/)
- **CaFE network figures (they conflict):**
  - One page shows 180,118 active artists, 850 organizations and 1,321 calls posted.
  - Another cites "over 5,700 art call listings" a year.
  - An older figure is "over 800" calls a year.
  - The login page says 1,200+ organizations; the "Why CaFE" page says nearly 1,000.
  - Sources: [CaFE feed](https://cafe.callforentry.org/feed/); [CaFE login](https://www.callforentry.org/login/); [Why CaFE](https://www.callforentry.org/how-it-works/why-cafe/)
- **Writing contests:**
  - P&W publishes a bimonthly Grants & Awards database of contests it has vetted (no total count shown).
  - Winning Writers monitors "over 200" poetry and prose contests with no entry fees.
  - Chill Subs once listed 1,200+ contests (older BetaList figure).
  - Sources: [P&W Grants & Awards](https://www.pw.org/grants); [Winning Writers](https://winningwriters.com/the-best-free-literary-contests); [BetaList](https://betalist.com/startups/chill-subs)

### Inferences
- **Estimated CaFE fee volume (2024): about US$3.8M.** Method: 186,083 entries × $20.22. This assumes the average applies across all entries; fee-free calls may already be in the average.
- **Film is the largest fee-driven open-call vertical.** FilmFreeway's 12,000+ festivals each take hundreds to thousands of paid submissions. Even 12,000 festivals × 300 submissions × $30 would be about $100M a year. This is purely illustrative with assumed inputs, not a sourced estimate.
- **Likely number of fee-charging writing contests:** in the low thousands, about 1,000–2,000. This combines the old Chill Subs figure with the Duotrope contest share. Low confidence.

### Gaps
- I found no published annual FilmFreeway submission count or fee volume. FilmFreeway is private and publishes neither.
- I found no count of photography competitions. Sources to try: PhotoContestInsider, Photo Contest Hub, Lens Culture.
- I could not verify the "9,000+" figure from the brief; the current homepage says 12,000+.

## 5. What is the total spend on submission, reading and entry fees?

### Takeaway
I found no aggregate estimate of submission-fee spend across literary magazines, contests or festivals. The available data points are:
- **Share charging fees:** about 13% of lit mags charge reading fees. That rises to 24% among the top 1,000 and 56% among the top 100.
- **Average visual art entry fee:** about $20 (CaFE).
- **Unit economics:** Submittable takes $0.99 + 5% per paid transaction, so a $3 fee nets the magazine about $1.86.
- **Example cost for a large magazine:** The Sun pays about $18,000 a year for Submittable, plus transaction fees.

### Cited Findings
- **Fee-charging rates (Chill Subs data via Lit Hub):** about 13% of all tracked lit mags charge fees, 24% of the top 1,000 and 56% of the top 100. Writers are nearly twice as likely to submit to fee-based magazines.
- **The Sun:**
  - Charges $2.50 per submission and pays about $18,000 a year for Submittable, plus transaction fees.
  - Paid submissions fell from about 36,000 to about 24,000 a year.
  - Source for this and the item above: [Lit Hub, May 7 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **Submittable transaction terms:**
  - $0.99 + 5% per transaction.
  - CLMP-member magazines get a plan at $39 a month ($290 a year).
  - On a $3 fee, the platform takes $1.14.
  - Source: [Melissa Olson Petrie, Dec 22 2020](https://melissaolsonpetrie.com/2020/12/22/submittable-follow-the-money-in-literary-magazine-publishing/) (older; pricing may have changed)
- **2018 survey of 15 poetry presses and 27 magazines:** submission fees made up "a sizable, integral portion" of funding. The sample is small and no exact percentage is given. — [The Millions, Jan 2018](https://themillions.com/2018/01/paying-to-play-on-solvency-and-submissions-fees-in-poetry-publishing.html)
- **CaFE 2024:** $20.22 average entry fee across 186,083 entries. — [CaFE](https://www.callforentry.org/looking-back-at-2024/)

### Inferences
- **Lit mag reading fees: about $5–15M a year (rough estimate).** Method: about 5,000–6,000 active magazines × 13% charging ≈ 650–800 magazines. For a range, take 2,000–20,000 paid submissions a year × $3 each.
- **Contest fees** ($15–30 typical) probably equal or exceed that. I have no source.
- **Film festival fees** are likely the largest pool, in the tens to hundreds of millions of dollars a year (unsourced).
- **The take-rate model matters.** Submittable takes a cut per transaction. A competitor with flat or lower fees could be a direct pitch to fee-charging magazines.

### Gaps
- I found no published industry-wide survey of fee revenue (CLMP, P&W or NEA).
- I found no FilmFreeway fee GMV. FilmFreeway publishes no transaction volumes.

## 6. What share of organizations use Submittable or another submission manager, versus email or Google Forms?

### Takeaway
Submittable says 11,000–13,000+ organizations use it, running 165K+ programs, with about 25M applications processed and $10B+ in funds managed. It has also shifted heavily toward grantmaking and CSR. I found no systematic data on the share of lit mags or arts orgs using Submittable versus email or Google Forms. The anecdotal evidence:
- Submittable dominates mid-to-large journals.
- Email remains common among small outlets.
- Alternatives include Duosuma (Duotrope), Moksha, Green Submissions and Subfolio.

### Cited Findings
- **Submittable's customer counts (they conflict, all undated marketing pages):**
  - Customer stories page: 11,000+ organizations.
  - Partners page: 12k+ organizations, 25M applications processed, $2B funds delivered by Submittable and partners. This page is about two years old.
  - Customer success page: over 6,000 organizations (likely stale).
  - Sources: [Submittable Customer Stories](https://www.submittable.com/customer-stories); [Submittable Partners](https://www.submittable.com/partners/); [Submittable Customer Success](https://www.submittable.com/solutions/customer-success)
- **Submittable features page:** 165K+ programs supported and $10B+ funds managed. — [Submittable Features](https://www.submittable.com/features)
- **Third-party Submittable figures:**
  - Software Advice: over 145,000 social impact programs and nearly 25M applications.
  - Another review: 13,000+ organizations (no primary source).
  - Sources: [Software Advice 2026](https://www.softwareadvice.com/nonprofit/submittable-profile/); [SmartThoughts review 2026](https://www.smartthoughts.net/post/submittable-grant-management-software-review-associations)
- **Submittable launched in 2010.** — [Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **Duotrope's listing criteria** name the accepted submission platforms: Duosuma (Duotrope's own manager), Submittable, Green Submissions and Moksha. — [Duotrope listing criteria](https://duotrope.com/about/listing-criteria.aspx); [Duosuma](https://duotrope.com/duosuma/)
- **Moksha** dates to about 2011. **Litmaglab** reviews 8+ submission managers (Subfolio, Submittable and others) and lists 14+ Submittable alternatives. — [Litmaglab: Moksha overview](https://litmaglab.substack.com/p/ep85-moksha-overview-a-submission); [Litmaglab: 8 submission systems 2025-26](https://litmaglab.substack.com/p/submission-management-systems-for-lit-mags-and-presses); [Litmaglab: 14 alternatives](https://litmaglab.substack.com/p/14-alternatives-to-submittable)
- **Anecdotal usage:**
  - A writer reports that "nearly half" of the publications they submitted to used Submittable.
  - Guides say Submittable is used by "the majority of mid-to-large journals", with email the main alternative.
  - Google Forms is used by some small magazines, with weak multi-reader workflow.
  - Sources: [search summary of writer blogs, incl. CMS Critic overview](https://cmscritic.com/3-submission-management-systems-for-literary-journals-an-overview); [Purdue OWL](https://owl.purdue.edu/owl/subject_specific_writing/creative_writing/writers/professional_resources/submitting_to_literary_magazines.html)
- **Film:** FilmFreeway is the de facto standard for festivals (12,000+). Withoutabox, the former incumbent, shut down in 2019. — [FilmFreeway](https://filmfreeway.com/); [Wikipedia: Withoutabox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withoutabox)
- **Visual art calls:** CaFE serves about 850–1,200 organizations. — [CaFE](https://www.callforentry.org/how-it-works/why-cafe/)

### Inferences
- **The submission-manager market splits by vertical:**
  - Film: FilmFreeway, near-monopoly.
  - US public art and juried exhibitions: CaFE.
  - Literary magazines and presses: Submittable, plus a long tail of Duosuma, Moksha, Green Submissions, Subfolio, email and Google Forms.
  - Grants and CSR: Submittable, plus enterprise grant tools.
- **Submittable's 11–13k organizations are mostly not literary.** The scale ($10B in funds, 165k programs) shows its centre of gravity is grantmaking and CSR. The arts-and-literary long tail may be under-served.
- **Estimated literary share:** perhaps 40–60% of active lit mags use Submittable, with the rest on email, Forms or smaller tools. This is an estimate from anecdotal sources only, with low confidence. A crawl of Duotrope or Chill Subs guidelines by submission method would give a real figure.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic measurement of submission-method share. Chill Subs reports having read 4,000+ guidelines ([Chill Subs blog](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/submissions-guidelines-feedback)) and may hold this data.
- I found no Submittable revenue or ARR figures, and no 2025–26 press release with updated counts.
- I found no adoption counts for Duosuma or Moksha.
