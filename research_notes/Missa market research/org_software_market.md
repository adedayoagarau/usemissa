# Organization-side submission, application and grant management software: market and vendors

Research date: 9 October 2026. Every figure below carries the date of its source. Figures from market-research firms, Latka, Growjo and review aggregators are estimates of mixed or low quality, and are flagged as such.

## Published market size estimates and CAGR (grant, submission, awards management)

### Takeaway
Grant management software is the best-documented category. Recent reports put it at about $1.8B–$2.6B (2023–2024 base years) and growing about 9–12% a year. Estimates for "awards management" ($0.56B–$0.77B base) and "submission management" ($0.5B vs $3.85B for 2025–2026) disagree badly enough that they cannot be used to size a literary or arts niche. None of these reports isolates literary magazines, presses, residencies or arts calls. That segment is a small slice and has to be sized bottom-up.

### Cited Findings
**Grant management software**
- Global Market Insights (report GMI15239, published Nov 2025): about $2.2B in 2024, rising to about $5.8B by 2034, a 10.2% CAGR for 2025–2034. — [GMI](https://www.gminsights.com/industry-analysis/grant-management-software-market)
- Technavio (Dec 2025): the market adds about $1.52B over 2024–2029 at a 10.4% CAGR. It names regulatory compliance and funder reporting mandates as the main driver. — [Technavio](https://www.technavio.com/report/grant-management-software-market-industry-analysis)
- Research and Markets (added Jan 2026): $2.58B in 2024, $2.88B in 2025 and $4.47B by 2029, an 11.6% CAGR. — [Research and Markets](https://www.researchandmarkets.com/reports/6227888/grant-management-software-market)
- Credence Research: $2.53B in 2024, about $5.55B by 2032, a 10.3% CAGR. — [Credence](https://www.credenceresearch.com/report/grant-management-software-market)
- Global Industry Analysts-style report (via GII): $1.8B in 2023, $3.4B by 2030, a 9.2% CAGR. — [GII](https://www.gii.co.jp/report/go1326010-grant-management-software.html)
- MarketsandMarkets (2019, dated): $1.1B in 2019, $1.9B by 2024, a 10.5% CAGR. — [MarketsandMarkets](https://www.marketsandmarkets.com/PressReleases/grant-management-software.asp)

**Awards management software**

**"Submission management software" (very low confidence)**
- Market Research Intellect: $504M in 2025, $1.57B by 2035, a 12% CAGR. — [MRI](https://www.marketresearchintellect.com/product/mission-management-systems-market/)
- Business Research Insights: $3.85B in 2026, $11.1B by 2035, a 12.5% CAGR. That is about 7x the MRI figure for the same label. — [BRI](https://www.businessresearchinsights.com/market-reports/submission-management-software-market-115473)
- Other reports using the same "submission/submittal" label cover construction submittals ($2.1B in 2025) and pharma eCTD ($1.2B in 2025). They are not comparable. — [Market Intelo](https://marketintelo.com/report/building-submittal-management-software-market)

### Inferences
- The figure to quote for grant management is "about $2–2.6B in 2024, growing about 10–12% a year (GMI Nov 2025; Research and Markets Jan 2026)". Cite it as a range, never as one number. These reports are paywalled and their methods are opaque, so treat them as low-to-medium confidence.
- Grant management is dominated by enterprise and foundation buyers: Blackbaud, Bonterra, Fluxx, Foundant/SmartSimple and Submittable. The literary, arts and festival segment Missa would target is a small fraction of these totals. A rough bottom-up anchor follows. Chill Subs tracks thousands of magazines; about 200 new magazines launched a year in 2020–2022 ([Lit Hub, May 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)). Small-org software spend runs $0–$3,000 a year (see pricing below). On those numbers, the English-language literary-magazine and press segment is probably low tens of millions of dollars a year at most, not billions. This is an inference, not a sourced figure.
- The "submission management" reports should not appear in investor or planning material. Their ~7x disagreement shows the category has no stable definition.

### Gaps
- No research firm sizes literary, arts-calls, residency or festival submission software on its own.
- I could not read the full reports or their methodology (paywalled). I did not find a Grand View or Verified Market Reports grant-management figure with a primary URL.

## Vendor profiles: pricing, customers, revenue, funding, acquisitions

### Takeaway
Submittable is the scaled incumbent in social-impact and grants software. It has raised $64.3M per Latka, closed a $47M Series C led by Accel-KKR in June 2022, bought WizeHive in August 2024, named a new CEO in July 2026, and has an estimated $35M–$67M in revenue. It has been moving up-market, away from literary magazines. Grant-management incumbents are consolidating under private equity: Bonterra/Apax, Foundant+SmartSimple, and Fluxx's acquisition of Grantseeker. The low end (Duosuma, Moksha, Zealous, Subfolio, Galley, Dapple) charges from $0 to about $80 a month or per submission. Mid-market awards tools (Award Force, Good Grants, OpenWater, Submit.com, Evalato) charge about $2,500–$8,000 a year.

### Inferences
- Pricing falls into three tiers:
  1. Literary and indie tools at $0–$80/month or per submission (Duosuma, Moksha, Subfolio, Zealous, Galley, Dapple, Submittable's CLMP plan).
  2. Mid-market awards and grants tools at roughly $2,500–$8,000/year plus onboarding (Award Force, Good Grants, OpenWater, Submit.com, Evalato, CaFÉ per-call).
  3. Enterprise grants platforms on custom quotes, often five figures (Submittable core, Fluxx, SmartSimple, Bonterra, Blackbaud).
- Submittable's grants, CSR and government focus, the WizeHive deal and the new CEO's AI and grants framing all point to the literary niche being a legacy segment for it. That leaves an opening for a literary-native tool.
- Fees on paid submissions are a competitive lever:
  - Submittable: $0.99 + 5%
  - Subfolio: 3.9% + $0.65
  - Duosuma: $0.10 + about $0.40 processing
  - Zealous: 2.2% + 20p, capped at £2
  - FilmFreeway: about 6% (unverified)

## How small literary magazines and residencies manage submissions today

### Takeaway
No formal survey (CLMP or other) of how magazines receive submissions turned up. Editor accounts and Chill Subs data show:
- Submittable is still the default for established magazines.
- Many small magazines use email, Google Forms, Tally or Airtable to avoid cost.
- A growing set of cheap literary-native tools is taking share at the margin, notably Duosuma and Subfolio.

### Cited Findings
- Editors talk about alternatives at conferences, but none has replaced Submittable. Before Submittable, magazines used email or post; some tried website-embedded tools and found them less user-friendly (The Writer, 14 Oct 2025). — [The Writer](https://www.writermag.com/get-published/the-publishing-industry/future-of-literary-magazines/3)
- Volume growth reported in the same piece:
  - SmokeLong Quarterly: 60–75 submissions a week in 2016, 100 a week in 2019.
  - Kenyon Review: just over 6,000 in a 6-week window in 2016–17, 7,200 in 2018–19.
  — [The Writer](https://www.writermag.com/get-published/the-publishing-industry/future-of-literary-magazines/3)
- Chill Subs data (Lit Hub, 7 May 2025, by Chill Subs cofounder Benjamin Davis). Note that Chill Subs is a platform with its own interest.
  - About 13% of tracked magazines charge submission fees: 24% of the top 1,000 and 56% of the top 100.
  - About 40 new magazines launched a year in 2000–2015, about 200 a year in 2020–2022.
  — [Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- Lit Mag Lab's 2025–26 roundup lists free and DIY routes (Google Forms, Tally, Fillout, Airtable, SubMgr) next to paid tools, which suggests they are in active use. — [Lit Mag Lab](https://litmaglab.substack.com/p/submission-management-systems-for-lit-mags-and-presses)
- Some editors have reportedly moved to Duosuma while others stay on Submission Manager. Some never left email because of cost (aggregated search summary of Lit Mag Lab and The Writer material). — [Lit Mag Lab: 13 alternatives](https://litmaglab.substack.com/p/13-alternatives-to-submittable)
- Visual arts calls and residencies commonly run on CaFÉ (per-call pricing) and Zealous; film festivals run on FilmFreeway. — [CaFÉ](https://www.callforentry.org/get-to-know-cafe-who-we-are-and-what-we-do); [Zealous](https://zealous.co/about/art-organization-submissions); [MovieMaker](https://www.moviemaker.com/filmfreeway/)

### Inferences
- The realistic switching market is the long tail of magazines that are on Submittable's CLMP plan, priced out of it, or still using email or forms. These buyers are very price-sensitive: they pay $0–$40 a month and are often volunteer-run.

### Gaps
- No CLMP or other survey of submission-tool market share was found. The share of magazines on Submittable vs Duosuma vs email is unknown.
- No residency-specific data was found (for example, Alliance of Artists Communities tooling).

## Pain points: Submittable price increases, fee splits, small-press complaints

### Takeaway
The complaints are consistent:
- rising subscription costs (a few hundred dollars a year a decade ago, about $1,500 a year now for one magazine, quotes near $10K a year)
- a $0.99 + 5% cut of paid submissions
- slow payouts
- limits on the basic plan
- lock-in

These costs are one reason magazines charge reading fees. Most evidence is anecdotal, and I found no dated public announcement of a Submittable literary price increase.

### Cited Findings
- "A number of editors… are reeling from recent Submittable fee bumps"; Submittable charges a flat fee and takes a cut of submission fees and limits unpaid submissions; "most magazines can't" afford it (The Writer, 14 Oct 2025). — [The Writer](https://www.writermag.com/get-published/the-publishing-industry/future-of-literary-magazines/3)
- The Sun pays about $18,000 a year for Submittable plus transaction fees. Its $2.50 fee cut paid submissions from about 36,000 to about 24,000 a year. A $3 fee nets a magazine about $1.86 (Lit Hub, May 2025). — [Lit Hub](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- One editor says Submittable now costs their magazine $1,500 a year, against "a few hundred" about ten years earlier. — [Lit Mag News Substack comments](https://litmagnews.substack.com/p/can-we-talk-about-submittable/comments) (secondary, via search summary)
- A reader comment (Cecilia Tan, 15 Sep 2025) says Submittable quoted about $10,000 a year even with a nonprofit discount. — [Lit Mag Lab](https://litmaglab.substack.com/p/submission-management-systems-for-lit-mags-and-presses)

- Cost examples: a $3 reading fee costs $1.14 on Submittable and a $15 contest entry costs $1.74. This comes from a competitor's advocacy article (Green Submissions). — [Green Submissions](https://greensubmissions.com/articles/what-submittable-costs.html)
- A 2020 blog post, "Follow the money," describes the 5% + $0.99 cut. — [Melissa Olson Petrie](https://melissaolsonpetrie.com/2020/12/22/submittable-follow-the-money-in-literary-magazine-publishing/)
- The CLMP plan's discount (38%) is measured against an unpublished list price. — [Submittable Help](https://submittable.help/en/articles/3512930-how-can-my-literary-organization-receive-a-discounted-clmp-plan)

### Inferences
- What small presses value: a low, predictable flat price, a low or no cut of fees, fast payouts, generous free-submission allowances and easy reader assignment. Duosuma (per-submission) and Zealous (capped commission) already sell on this. Missa would need to beat both on price, or win on the integrated writer side.
- Submittable's pricing for literary orgs is opaque: the public page shows no prices and the CLMP plan depends on membership. Transparent pricing is a cheap differentiator.
