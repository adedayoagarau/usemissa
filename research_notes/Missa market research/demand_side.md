# Demand side: how many writers and artists submit to open calls, and what they spend

Research date: 2026-10-09. Every figure carries its publication or data date. Older data is flagged. Estimates are labelled **ESTIMATE** with the method. Duotrope's own pages (duotrope.com/about/statistics.aspx, /news/announcement.aspx) returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch, so Duotrope figures come from search-index excerpts of those pages and should be re-checked by hand.

## 1. How many professional and serious amateur writers, poets, visual artists, photographers and filmmakers are there?

### Takeaway
The US has about 2.8M artists in the labour force (2024). The occupations closest to Missa's users are fine artists (341k), writers and authors (266k) and photographers (217k): about 824k people whose main job is one of these. Comparable official counts are much smaller elsewhere (Canada 158k artists in 2016; Australia just under 50k practising professional artists in 2024). None of these counts includes serious amateurs or people whose art is a second job, and those are a large share of people who submit.

### Cited Findings
**United States**
- 2024: 2.8 million artists in the US labour force. Largest artist occupations: designers 1.0M, fine artists 341,000, architects 321,000, writers 266,000, musicians 235,000, producers 234,000, photographers 217,000. In the NEA/NASERC definitions, "fine artists" includes art directors and animators, "writers" means writers and authors, and the count includes unemployed people seeking work in the occupation. — [NEA/NASERC Arts Data Profile report a1-report-202605 (May 2026)](https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/a1-report-202605.pdf)
- 2022: about 2.7M artists in the labour force; fine artists 331,000; writers 262,000. — [NEA a1 report (Sept 2024)](https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/a1-report-202409.pdf)
- Older series, 2005–2009 (OLD, not comparable): 2.1M artists; fine artists, art directors and animators 212,236; writers and authors 197,768. — [NEA Artists in the Workforce exec summary](https://arts.gov/sites/default/files/ArtistsInWorkforce_ExecSum.pdf)
- BLS OOH: craft and fine artists' median pay was $56,260 in 2024. Writers and authors fall in "media and communication" (group median $70,300, May 2024). Photographers fall in "media and communication equipment workers" (group median $56,480, May 2024). — [BLS OOH Arts & Design](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/arts-and-design); [BLS OOH Media & Communication](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication)

**Canada**
- 2016 census (OLD): 158,100 artists, 0.87% of the labour force, counted by the occupation where they spent the most time. 2011 NHS: 136,600. 2001: 130,700. The methods changed, so these numbers are not a trend line. — [Canada Council / Hill Strategies, A Statistical Profile of Artists in Canada in 2016 (Mar 2019, revised)](https://canadacouncil.ca/research/research-library/2019/03/a-statistical-profile-of-artists-in-canada-in-2016)

**Australia**
- *Artists as Workers* (Throsby & Petetskaya for Creative Australia, May 2024): "slightly less than 50,000" practising professional artists. The number has stayed broadly flat since the 1990s. — [Creative Australia news release (2024)](https://creative.gov.au/news-events/news/major-report-shows-economic-conditions-artists-odds-value-we-place-arts); [Macquarie Lighthouse (May 2024)](https://lighthouse.mq.edu.au/article/may-2024/creative-work-doesnt-pay-the-bills-new-artists-survey)
- Earlier *Making Art Work* (2017, OLD): 48,000 practising artists, including 8,600 visual artists and 7,900 writers. — [Creative Australia fact sheet](https://creative.gov.au/wp-content/uploads/2021/07/artists-and-the-arts-fact-shee-54f5f36cb6dbc.pdf)

**United Kingdom**
- ALCS author earnings survey 2018 (OLD): 2,696 complete responses. The top 10% of authors earn about 70% of the profession's total earnings. A 2022 repeat ran but I did not find a response count. — [Kretschmer et al. 2019, CopyrightEvidence wiki](https://copyrightevidence.org/wiki/index.php/Kretschmer,_Gavaldon,_Miettinen_and_Singh_(2019)); [The Bookseller](https://thebookseller.com/news/alcs-commissions-new-survey-on-writers-earnings)

**Writing education and community (US)**
- MFA programs: 244 in 2016, up from 156 in 2008. Roughly 3,000 new MFA graduates a year (Lit Hub, 2017, OLD). An AWP job report estimate puts it at about 4,000 advanced creative-writing degrees a year. — [Lit Hub, MFA by the Numbers (2017)](https://lithub.com/mfa-by-the-numbers-on-the-eve-of-awp/)
- Graduate and undergraduate creative writing programs together produce about 14,000 new degree holders a year. AWP membership was 50,000 (Narrative Magazine, circa 2017, OLD). — [Narrative Magazine](https://www.narrativemagazine.com/node/388027/printable/print)
- Poets & Writers magazine: readership 100,000+ and paid subscribers 42,000+ (2024 media kit). The 2022 kit claimed 60,000 paid subscribers, so the two sources conflict. 78% of readers say P&W is their main source of writing and publishing information. — [P&W media kit 2024](https://www.pw.org/files/pw_mediakit_2024_0.pdf); [P&W media kit 2022](https://m.pw.org/files/pw_mediakit_2022.pdf)
- NaNoWriMo peaked at roughly 400k+ participants: 413,295 in 2022, though another source gives 427,653 for 2021. The nonprofit shut down in April 2025. — [Wikipedia: National Novel Writing Month](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE, US core professional pool: about 0.8M.** Method: fine artists 341k + writers 266k + photographers 217k (2024 NEA). Filmmakers sit inside "producers and directors" (234k), which also includes TV and commercial staff. This counts primary occupation only.
- The aspiring and amateur writing population is much larger than the occupational count. NaNoWriMo alone drew about 400k people a year at its peak. Treat it as the top of a funnel, not as active submitters.
- English-speaking markets outside the US (UK, Canada, Australia) add roughly 25–35% to the US professional base. **ESTIMATE**, method: Canada 158k + Australia about 50k; the UK is probably on the scale of Canada or larger, but I found no comparable DCMS occupational count.

### Gaps
- No current UK DCMS or Arts Council England headcount of writers or visual artists was retrieved.
- No 2021-census Canadian artist count was found.
- No current AWP membership figure, and no MFA graduate count newer than 2017.
- No count of serious amateurs, meaning people who submit but are not employed as artists.

## 2. How many people actively submit? (platform, magazine, contest, residency and grant proxies)

### Takeaway
The large submission platforms each reach hundreds of thousands to low millions of applicants. Submittable had 1.18M applicants in 2025, but across all program types, not only arts. FilmFreeway claims 2M+ filmmakers, an undated vendor figure. CaFE had about 145k registered artists (2023). Chill Subs had 87k users in June 2026. Individual flagship opportunities draw thousands each:
- Creative Capital: 4,500–5,700 applications.
- Guggenheim: about 3,500.
- MacDowell: 1,700–2,400 per cycle.
- NEA Creative Writing Fellowships: about 2,000.
- ACE DYCP: about 1,900 per round.
- Clarkesworld: about 14,800 submissions a year.

- A Duotrope-relayed stat says The New Yorker accepted 0.14% of 1,447 unsolicited submissions in a year. **Flag:** this counts only submissions logged by Duotrope users, not The New Yorker's real inflow. — [Women Who Submit (Jun 2021)](https://womenwhosubmitlit.org/2021/06/16/submissions-the-harsh-reality-and-how-to-improve-your-odds/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE, English-language literary submitters who use a tracking or discovery tool: about 150k–300k.** Method: Chill Subs has 87k users. Duotrope's paying base is undisclosed, but it is the long-standing paid incumbent, so it is plausibly tens of thousands. The Submission Grinder is free and has an unknown user count. Users overlap across tools.
- **ESTIMATE, total active literary submitters in English: about 300k–600k.** Method: tool users are a subset of submitters. P&W has 42–60k paid subscribers and 100k+ readers. The Sun alone gets 24–36k submissions a year. About 200 new magazines launch each year. Low confidence.
- Visual and film applicants are a separate, comparably large pool. CaFE has about 145k registered artists, and FilmFreeway claims 2M+ (vendor, likely cumulative registrations). Submittable's 1.18M applicants in 2025 is an upper-bound signal for people applying to anything through one platform in one year.
- Flagship US cross-discipline fellowships each draw about 2k–6k applicants. That is the "serious professional" core: probably tens of thousands of unique individuals across all of them. **ESTIMATE**, low confidence, because applicants overlap heavily across programs.

### Gaps
- No public Duotrope subscriber count, Submission Grinder user total, or current CaFE count (newer than 2023).
- Bridport, Pushcart and BOA entry counts were not found.
- No annual submission totals for The New Yorker, Ploughshares, Granta or Tin House.
- Hedgebrook and Yaddo applicant counts were not retrieved.
- No unique-person count across Submittable (applicants may be double-counted across programs).

## 3. What do submitters spend on fees and tools?

### Takeaway
Fees per submission are small: a median of about $3 for journals and about $20 for contests. Fee-charging is concentrated at prestigious magazines (13% of all tracked magazines charge, but 56% of the top 100). An active writer making about 100 submissions a year plausibly spends a few hundred dollars on fees. Willingness to pay for tools is proven: Duotrope charged about $5 a month or $50 a year from 2013 to 2026 and raised to **$6 a month or $60 a year on June 1, 2026**. That is the same price as Missa Plus.

### Cited Findings
- **Duotrope pricing.** From June 1, 2026 (8 AM MDT), Duotrope costs USD $6.00 a month or $60.00 a year. Existing subscribers move to the new price at their first charge on or after July 1, 2026. Prices had been unchanged since paid plans launched in 2013. The NZD notice gives old prices of NZ$8 and NZ$80, which implies USD $5 and $50; the USD figure is my inference. Group discounts: 25% off; 33% off for groups of 50 or more; 50% off for 100 or more. — [Duotrope announcement (search excerpt; direct fetch blocked)](https://duotrope.com/news/announcement.aspx); [NZD version](https://duotrope.com/news/announcement.aspx?cur=nzd)
- **Duotrope fee report.** Among literary journal listings, 1,937 (73.15%) never charge fees and 335 (12.65%) always charge; the rest charge optionally or sometimes. The median fee paid is $3.00 and the average is $3.89. The median contest fee is $20. The page is undated (live report). — [Duotrope Submission Fees Report](https://duotrope.com/stats/submission-fees-report.aspx)
- **Chill Subs data (May 2025):**
  - 13% of tracked magazines charge fees; 24% of the top 1,000 and 56% of the top 100 do.
  - Writers are "nearly twice as likely" to submit to a fee-based magazine than a free one.
  - A $3 fee nets the magazine $1.86 after platform costs.
  - The Sun charges $2.50.
  - One writer's fee rose from $3 to $5. At 100 submissions a year that comes to about $300, yielding 2–3 acceptances.
  - A piece typically takes about 20 submissions to land.

  — [Lit Hub (May 7, 2025)](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- **Typical fee levels.** Journal fees hover around $3 for 3–6 poems. Book-manuscript contests cost about $25. The average contest fee is $15–20. — [Poetry Foundation](https://poetryfoundation.org/poetry-news/78822/pending-the-fraught-practice-of-the-poetry-submission-fee)
- **Contest fee trend (OLD).** P&W found average contest entry fees rose 9% from 2004 to 2014, and by $0.54 in 2015. — [P&W, Anatomy of Awards (Sep/Oct 2016)](https://www.pw.org/content/anatomy_of_awards_septemberoctober_2016)
- **Book publication costs.** In Rachel Mennies' survey covering 105 poetry books, nearly all the poets paid something out of pocket to publish, up to $3,000. — [The Millions](https://themillions.com/?p=100586)
- **Attitudes (2015, OLD, small sample).** In a Malahat Review survey of about 100 writers, 40% refuse to submit to fee-charging magazines and 30% pay "begrudgingly." In a 2015 sample of 50 Canadian and US journals, 12 charged fees, 37 did not and 1 made the fee optional. Most fees were $3 or less. — [Malahat Review, Publishing Tips (May 2015)](https://www.malahatreview.ca/tips/may2015.html)
- **Bridport 2025 entry fees:** £11–£14 per entry. — [P&W listing](https://www.pw.org/print/1104069)
- **Chill Subs paid tier.** About 10% of members use fee scholarships. I found no Chill Subs paid-tier subscriber count. — [Lit Hub (May 2025)](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)

### Inferences
- **ESTIMATE, annual fee spend per active literary submitter: about $50–$400.**
  - Method, journals only: 30–100 submissions a year × 25–40% sent to fee-charging venues (writers favour fee venues about 2:1, and fees cluster at top magazines) × a $3–$4 average fee gives roughly $25–$160.
  - Add 2–10 contests at a $20 median, about $40–$200.
  - The upper end matches the $300-a-year anecdote in Lit Hub.
- **ESTIMATE, visual and film applicants likely spend more per application:** $25–$75 is typical for juried shows and festivals. No survey data was retrieved, so this is unverified and should be checked against CaFE and FilmFreeway fee listings.
- Missa Plus at $60 a year is about 15–100% of a literary submitter's yearly fee spend by these estimates. It matches Duotrope's new price, so price is not a differentiator against the incumbent.

### Gaps
- No survey measuring average annual spend per writer on submission or contest fees; all per-writer totals here are estimates or anecdotes.
- No Duotrope subscriber count or revenue figure. No Chill Subs paid-tier conversion data.
- No average-fee data for FilmFreeway or CaFE.

## 4. Acceptance rates and submissions per writer per year

### Takeaway
Acceptance rates at competitive venues are low:
- Magazine "top tier": under 1%.
- Flagship fellowships: about 1–9%. Creative Capital is about 1–2.4%, NEA under 2%, Guggenheim about 6%, MacDowell 6–9%.
- UK DYCP: about 20%.

An active literary submitter makes tens of submissions a year, often about 100, and needs about 20 per acceptance. That volume is the core case for a tracker.

### Cited Findings
- About 20 submissions per acceptance. An active writer sends about 100 submissions a year for 2–3 acceptances. — [Lit Hub (May 2025)](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)
- Chill Subs logs about 4.4k tracked submissions a month (Jan 2026). Across Jan–May 2026, users grew about 12k and submissions about 55k. That is a lifetime average of about 4 tracked submissions per user (355,581 ÷ 87,244), which is a mix of active and inactive users. — [Chill Subs roundups](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/may-2026-roundup)
- Clarkesworld: about 1.6 submissions per author per year (2009). Returning authors are 38% of authors and 48% of submissions (2021). — [Neil Clarke](https://neil-clarke.com/2009-the-year-in-slush/); [stats](https://neil-clarke.com/category/stats/)
- Submission Grinder market pages show crowd-sourced acceptance rates over the past 12 months, for example: Pulp Asylum about 6% (147 reports), Third Flatiron about 9%. Samples are small. No site-wide totals were found. — [The Grinder](https://thegrinder.diabolicalplots.com/Market?id=45)
- A Duotrope-reported New Yorker acceptance rate of 0.14% (Duotrope-user sample). — [Women Who Submit (2021)](https://womenwhosubmitlit.org/2021/06/16/submissions-the-harsh-reality-and-how-to-improve-your-odds/)

### Inferences
- The heavy users who would pay for tracking are the roughly 100-submissions-a-year writers. Platform averages (about 4 lifetime submissions per Chill Subs user) show that most registered users are light or occasional. **ESTIMATE:** perhaps 10–20% of tool users are heavy submitters. This is unverified; the method is the gap between the 100-a-year anecdote and the platform average.

### Gaps
- No Duotrope site-wide statistics: the page was blocked, and Duotrope's per-market acceptance data is behind a paywall.
- No Submission Grinder aggregate totals.
- No survey distribution of submissions per writer per year.
