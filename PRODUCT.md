# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Missa serves anonymous visitors and creators discovering, comparing, saving, preparing, and tracking creative opportunities; organization administrators publishing and managing calls; reviewers making consequential decisions with limited context; and content-quality or platform operators resolving identity, source, taxonomy, publication, and system-state issues. Creator workflows must be mobile-first and calm under deadline pressure. Organization workflows may be denser and desktop-oriented, while occasional reviewers should need almost no training.

## Product Purpose

Missa is the trusted opportunity layer for creative work. It connects source-grounded opportunity discovery to preparation, submission, review, decisions, delivery, and outcomes while preserving the identity, facts, evidence, status, and available actions of an Opportunity across contexts. Success means users can determine whether an Opportunity is relevant and trustworthy, take the correct next action, and return to an accurate record without reconstructing the process across disconnected tools.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, and human. Missa should feel editorial without becoming nostalgic, capable without becoming enterprise-heavy, and trustworthy without relying on alarm colors. Public and creator surfaces should communicate quiet confidence and relief. Organization surfaces should communicate control and competence. Reviewer surfaces should support focused, low-friction work.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS chrome, anonymous dashboard cards, and decorative glass effects.
- Submittable-style enterprise heaviness, slow setup, and unnecessary operational friction.
- Unexplained scores, badges, confidence labels, or status changes.
- Paper, parchment, cream, beige, or literary-journal facsimiles used as a shortcut for editorial character.
- Mismatched component families, invented affordances for standard tasks, or different interaction grammar across public, creator, and organization surfaces.
- Community ratings, inferred statistics, or discovery provenance presented as Organization-authored truth.

## Design Principles

1. Preserve object identity and truth across every surface. Context may change composition and available actions, not the underlying facts.
2. Explain every status, match, change, and unavailable action in plain language.
3. Use one product grammar with density appropriate to the user’s task: spacious for creator discovery, compact for frequent organization operations.
4. Make authority visible through the Organization and its official destination while keeping internal discovery provenance out of the public interface.
5. Design failure, sparse data, long content, responsive reflow, permissions, and recovery as primary states rather than late exceptions.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA is the baseline. Public and creator experiences must support touch, keyboard, assistive technology, responsive zoom and mobile completion. Controls should ordinarily provide at least 44px targets; compact 36px organization controls are reserved for frequent desktop users. Meaning cannot depend on color alone. Reduced-motion alternatives are required, focus must remain visible, and labels cannot disappear when navigation collapses.
