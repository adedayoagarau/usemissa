# Missa Must Win on Breadth, Not Price

Missa is entering a real but modest creator market. Its venture-scale upside sits on the organization side and in payments, not in $60 subscriptions. On the creator side, roughly **0.7–2.6 million people a year** send work to open calls in English. About **200k–500k** of them use a discovery or tracking tool. Writers have proven they will pay $6–$10 a month. Even so, a realistic three-year Plus business is about **$15k–$375k ARR (base about $95k)**. That is because only heavy submitters pay. The most direct comparable, Chill Subs, took four years to reach **87,244 users** without outside money ([Chill Subs, May 2026 roundup, published 4 Jun 2026](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/may-2026-roundup)). On the organization side, a bottom-up count of literary magazines, residencies, visual-art call organizers and small individual-artist funders gives a serviceable market of about **$5M–$45M a year (base about $15M)**. That market is under-served: **Submittable**, the incumbent (estimated revenue $35M–$67M, $47M Series C), has moved toward grants and CSR ([Latka, updated 3 Oct 2026](https://getlatka.com/companies/submittable); [Accel-KKR, 13 Jun 2022](https://www.accel-kkr.com/submittable-secures-47-million-in-series-c-funding-led-by-akkr-to-accelerate-social-impact-programs/)). Price is no longer a lever: on **1 June 2026, Duotrope raised to exactly $6/month or $60/year**, its first increase since 2013 ([Duotrope announcement, Jun 2026](https://duotrope.com/news/announcement.aspx)). So Missa has to win on four things Duotrope lacks: a free tier, coverage across writing, visual art and residencies, current fee and eligibility data, and a two-sided link to organizations. Missa's catalogue is already competitive in size: **6,011 published calls from 2,526 organizations**, and **3,786 open or opening soon** (Missa production database, 9 Oct 2026). Traction is at zero: **24 product sign-ups and $0 revenue**. In any pitch, the catalogue is the asset to lead with, and traction is the gap to name honestly.

## How this report labels its numbers

Every external figure is cited inline with its source and the date of the source, or the date it was retrieved where the page is undated. Three labels apply. **ESTIMATE** means a figure calculated in this report or in the research notes from stated inputs. **Low confidence** marks figures resting on vendor marketing, third-party aggregators, search-engine excerpts, or small samples. **Internal** marks Missa's own numbers, counted from the production database on 9 October 2026. Several Duotrope pages returned HTTP 403 to direct fetch, so Duotrope figures come from search-index excerpts of Duotrope's own pages. Check them by hand before an investor sees them. This report replaces the market-size and competitor sections of the earlier pitch data pack (`docs/pitch/README.md`), which overstated the creator market at $300–600M TAM and $30–60M SAM and used stale competitor counts.

## Demand: up to 2.6 million people submit each year, and the payers are a heavy-submitting minority

### The workforce is large, but occupational counts miss the amateurs who submit most

The US had **2.8 million artists in the labour force in 2024**. The occupations closest to Missa's users were **fine artists at 341,000, writers and authors at 266,000, and photographers at 217,000**, plus 234,000 producers and directors ([NEA/NASERC Arts Data Profile, May 2026](https://www.arts.gov/sites/default/files/a1-report-202605.pdf)). The first three sum to a US core of about **824,000 people** whose main job is one of these crafts. Official counts elsewhere are smaller and older: Canada counted **158,100 artists in its 2016 census** ([Canada Council / Hill Strategies, Mar 2019](https://canadacouncil.ca/research/research-library/2019/03/a-statistical-profile-of-artists-in-canada-in-2016)), and Australia has "slightly less than 50,000" practising professional artists ([Creative Australia, May 2024](https://creative.gov.au/news-events/news/major-report-shows-economic-conditions-artists-odds-value-we-place-arts)). These counts leave out the serious amateurs and second-job artists who make up much of any slush pile. The amateur funnel is far wider: NaNoWriMo drew **413,295 participants in 2022** before the nonprofit shut down in April 2025 ([Wikipedia: NaNoWriMo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Novel_Writing_Month)). Poets & Writers reports **100,000+ readers and 42,000+ paid subscribers**, and 78% of its readers call it their main source of publishing information ([P&W media kit 2024](https://www.pw.org/files/pw_mediakit_2024_0.pdf)). Its 2022 kit claimed 60,000 paid subscribers, so the subscriber figure is low confidence.

### Platform and flagship-program counts show who actually applies

Submission platforms give the best direct signal of active applicants:

- **Submittable** handled **1,184,594 applicants across 28,931 programs in 2025** ([Submittable, 2025 year in review, 2 Jan 2026](https://www.submittable.com/blog/contentful-blog-2025-our-year-in-review)). Most of those programs are grants, scholarships and CSR, not arts calls.
- **CaFE** reported **175,000 active artists and 3.5M submissions** in FY25 ([CaFE Info Packet 2025](https://media.callforentry.org/2025/03/26141905/CaFE-Info-Packet-2025.pdf)).
- **FilmFreeway** claims to reach "over two million filmmakers" across 12,000+ festivals ([FilmFreeway, undated, retrieved Oct 2026](https://filmfreeway.com/pages/how-it-works)). This is vendor marketing and probably a cumulative count, so it is low confidence.
- **ArtConnect** says it serves "about a million artists a year" ([ArtConnect press, undated](https://magazine.artconnect.com/press)). This is self-reported and low confidence.

Flagship programs show how much demand concentrates on a few prizes:

- Creative Capital: **5,653 applications for 55 awards** in 2025, about 1% ([Creative Capital, 21 Jan 2025](https://creative-capital.org/press/announcing-the-2025-creative-capital-awards)).
- Guggenheim: nearly **3,500 applicants for 198 fellowships** in 2025 ([Guggenheim Foundation, Apr 2025](https://gf.org/stories/announcing-the-2025-guggenheim-fellows)).
- MacDowell: **2,222 applicants for 194 places** in spring–summer 2025 ([MacDowell](https://www.macdowell.org/news/macdowell-awards-spring-summer-fellowships-to-artists-working-across-disciplines)).
- Clarkesworld: **14,805 submissions in 2025**, excluding banned AI work ([Neil Clarke, stats](https://neil-clarke.com/category/stats/)).
- Arts Council England Project Grants: **9,642 applications for 3,084 awards** in 2023/24 ([ACE Annual Report 2023/24](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/67290789fbd69e1861921b18/Arts_Council_Annual_Report_and_Accounts_202324__1_.pdf)).

These pools overlap heavily, so they cannot be summed. They show that applying is a volume game with acceptance rates of 1–9% at flagship programs.

### Usage, spend and acceptance rates describe the customer

Chill Subs is the clearest proxy for a modern writer tool. It had **18,000+ registered writers in February 2024** ([Publishers Weekly, 12 Feb 2024](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/94166-chill-subs-wants-to-take-the-angst-out-of-submissions.html)), **75,000 users in January 2026** ([Chill Subs](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/january-2026-roundup)), and **87,244 users with 355,581 tracked submissions** in its May 2026 data, published 4 June 2026 ([Chill Subs](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/may-2026-roundup)). That is about 4.8x in 27 months, or roughly 4% a month in 2026. The same figures give only **about 4 tracked submissions per user over the life of the account**. Most registered users are occasional, not the heavy users who would pay.

The heavy user is easy to describe. Chill Subs data published in Literary Hub says a piece typically needs **about 20 submissions to land**. An active writer sends **about 100 submissions a year for 2–3 acceptances**. At $3–$5 a fee, that comes to **about $300 a year in reading fees** ([Lit Hub, "The Rise of the Submission Industrial Complex," 7 May 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)).

Duotrope's live fee report puts the **median journal fee paid at $3.00 (average $3.89) and the median contest fee at $20**. It also shows **73% of journal listings never charge** ([Duotrope Submission Fees Report, undated live page](https://duotrope.com/stats/submission-fees-report.aspx)). On the visual side, CaFE reports a **$20.22 average entry fee across 186,083 entries in 2024** ([CaFE, Looking Back at 2024](https://www.callforentry.org/looking-back-at-2024/)). Missa's own catalogue shows a **$15 median fee across 1,421 USD-priced calls and 1,797 no-fee calls** (Internal, 9 Oct 2026).

**ESTIMATE:** an active literary submitter spends about **$50–$400 a year** on fees. That range combines 30–100 journal submissions, 25–40% of them to fee-charging venues at $3–$4 each, with 2–10 contests at $20. At $60, Missa Plus is therefore about 15–100% of a heavy writer's yearly fee bill. That is a meaningful ask, and it explains why free alternatives keep appearing.

Low incomes limit willingness to pay. A UK survey of 271 visual artists found a **median artistic income of £12,500**, and **56% earned under £5,000 from art sales** ([Creative United, Nov 2025](https://www.creativeunited.org.uk/services/understanding-uk-artists-livelihoods-the-2024-survey/)). The NORC National Survey of Artists found **11% hold three or more jobs** ([ICPSR, Oct 2025](https://www.icpsr.umich.edu/sites/icpsr/posts/shared/national-survey-of-artists-reveals-full-extent-of-americas-creative-workforce-and-their-economic-insecurity)). This NORC figure came from a search excerpt and is low confidence.

## Supply: about 10,000–18,000 organizations outside film issue calls, and most arts money never passes through an open call

### Counting the organizations

The supply side is fragmented and churns heavily:

- **Literary outlets.** Duotrope lists **7,808 current publishers and agents** against **22,187 closed or defunct listings** ([Duotrope catalog, undated](https://duotrope.com/search/catalog.aspx)). Chill Subs' homepage shows **5,176 literary magazines** ([Chill Subs, undated](https://www.chillsubs.com/)). The curated, quality tier is about **670–1,000 magazines** ([Poets & Writers](https://www.pw.org/); [P&W guide](https://www.pw.org/content/literary_magazines)). **ESTIMATE:** about 5,000–8,000 active English-language outlets that take unsolicited work, of which perhaps 5,000–6,000 are magazines.
- **New launches.** New literary magazines ran at about **40 a year from 2000 to 2015, then 200 in 2020, 201 in 2021 and 194 in 2022** ([Lit Hub, 7 May 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)). That fivefold rise adds new organizations to discover every year. It also means constant churn for anyone selling them software.
- **Residencies.** TransArtists lists "more than 1700" ([TransArtists, undated](https://www.transartists.org/en/residencies)). Res Artis has about **700 members in 80+ countries** ([Res Artis](https://resartis.org/)). **ESTIMATE:** about 1,500–2,000 programs globally, of which about 300–500 are in the US.
- **Visual-art calls.** CaFE serves **850–1,200 organizations**, and different CaFE pages give between 1,321 and "over 5,700" call listings a year ([CaFE feed](https://cafe.callforentry.org/feed/); [Why CaFE](https://www.callforentry.org/how-it-works/why-cafe/)). EntryThingy showed **1,338 calls** in October 2026 ([EntryThingy](https://app.entrythingy.com/calls_list)).
- **Film.** FilmFreeway hosts **12,000+ festivals and screenplay contests** ([FilmFreeway](https://filmfreeway.com/)).
- **Missa's own count.** Missa tracks **10,492 organizations**, of which **2,526 have a published call** (Internal, 9 Oct 2026). These are the organizations the roadmap portal would first sell to.

**ESTIMATE:** adding the segment ranges gives about 10,000–18,000 organizations outside film. The ranges are 5,000–8,000 literary outlets, 1,500–2,000 residencies, 2,000–4,000 visual-art call organizers, and an assumed 1,000–4,000 small funders. This total excludes film's 12,000+ festivals. Missa's 10,492 tracked organizations fall inside the range.

### Following the money

The money is large in aggregate but mostly does not reach individuals through open calls:

- **US giving.** Arts, culture and humanities giving reached **$27.31B in 2025, up 7.5%** ([Giving USA 2026, Jun 2026](https://givingusa.org/giving-usa-charitable-giving-rose-to-617-20-billion-in-2025-surpassing-the-600-billion-mark-for-the-first-time/)).
- **Foundations.** Foundations gave about **$4B to arts and culture in 2023, a record-low 6% of foundation dollars**, down from 13% in 2010. **52%** of those arts dollars came in grants of $500K or more ([GIA/Candid, Foundation Grants to Arts and Culture 2023](https://reader.giarts.org/read/foundation-grants-to-arts-and-culture-2023)).
- **US public funding** is small and contested. The NEA was held at **$207M for FY2026**, and the President's FY2027 request proposes **$29M**, which would close the agency ([NASAA, Feb 2026](https://nasaa-arts.org/newsletter/2026-nasaa-notes-issues/february-2026-nasaa-notes/nea-funded-for-fy2026/)). State arts agencies received **$646.0M in FY2026, down 5.9%** ([NASAA FY2026 revenues report](https://artsboard.wisconsin.gov/Documents/FY2026-State-Arts-Agency-Revenues-Report_final-updated.pdf)).
- **Canada.** The Canada Council granted **C$284.4M to 5,305 recipients in 2024–25** ([Canada Council](https://canadacouncil.ca/funding)).

Large councils run their own systems. The opening for a submission portal is therefore the long tail of small foundations, magazines, residencies and arts nonprofits, not national funders.

Fees are the money that actually passes through open calls, and nobody measures them in aggregate. Three data points give a sense of scale:

- **CaFE: about $3.8M in visual-art entry fees in 2024.** ESTIMATE: 186,083 entries × $20.22.
- **Literary reading fees: about $5–15M a year.** ESTIMATE, low confidence: roughly 650–800 fee-charging magazines × 2,000–20,000 paid submissions × about $3.
- **Film festival fees: "tens to hundreds of millions" a year.** This is illustrative and unsourced, since FilmFreeway publishes no transaction volume.

Fee economics are thin. On Submittable, a **$3 fee nets the magazine about $1.86** ([Lit Hub, 7 May 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)), because the platform charges **$0.99 + 5% per transaction** ([Green Submissions, citing Submittable Help, Jul 2026](https://greensubmissions.com/articles/what-submittable-costs.html)).

Three patterns stand out. First, **every creator-facing writer database is bootstrapped, user-funded or donation-funded**. Only the organization-paid platforms (Submittable, and FilmFreeway through Backstage) have institutional capital. Writers pay, but not at a scale that has attracted investors, and nobody has proven a venture outcome from creator subscriptions alone.

Second, the **category is raising prices on existing users** rather than growing the pie. Chill Subs' paid tier roughly doubled from $5 a month in 2024 to $10 in 2026 ([PW, Feb 2024](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/by-topic/industrynews/publisher-news/article/94166-chill-subs-wants-to-take-the-angst-out-of-submissions.html); [memberships](https://support.chillsubs.com/memberships)). FilmFreeway Gold rose about 36%, and Duotrope rose 20%.

Third, **visual artists are used to paying nothing to the platform**, because organizers pay. A creator subscription will sell to writers more easily than to visual artists. That is a direct argument for monetizing the visual-art side through organizations instead.

About 40 staff were reportedly cut ([GeekWire, 2024, search excerpt](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/social-impact-software-platform-submittable-acquires-wizehive-cuts-staff/)).
- **July 2026:** named **Ben Chodor CEO**, with an AI and grant-workflow agenda ([GlobeNewswire, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/28/3334484/0/en/submittable-announces-ben-chodor-as-new-ceo.html)).
- **Revenue:** estimates range from **$34.9M** ([Growjo](https://growjo.com/company/Submittable)) to **$66.6M** (Latka estimate, Oct 2024) ([Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/submittable)). Both are low confidence.
- **Funds managed:** **$4.37B in 2025** ([Submittable, Jan 2026](https://www.submittable.com/blog/contentful-blog-2025-our-year-in-review)).

That profile, with grants and CSR at the centre of gravity, makes literary magazines a legacy segment for Submittable. Editors' complaints confirm it:

These acquirers form a plausible exit set for a vertical arts-and-literary portal. None of them owns a creator-side audience.

## Six trends favour a current, two-sided directory over a static database

**Reading fees cluster at the top.** About **13% of tracked magazines charge, rising to 24% of the top 1,000 and 56% of the top 100**, and writers are "nearly twice as likely" to submit to fee-based magazines ([Lit Hub, 7 May 2025](https://lithub.com/the-rise-of-the-submission-industrial-complex/)). Benjamin Davis of Chill Subs calls it "not a slippery slope. It's a cliff." Fees hit hardest outside the US: a $3–$5 fee can equal a day's wages in India. About **10% of Chill Subs members use fee scholarships** (same source). For writers deciding where to spend a limited budget, accurate fee, waiver and free-window fields are decision data.

**AI submission floods are pushing gatekeeping upstream.** Clarkesworld closed submissions on 20 February 2023 after receiving **500 machine-written stories against 700 legitimate ones** that month ([NPR, 24 Feb 2023](https://www.npr.org/2023/02/24/1159286436/ai-chatbot-chatgpt-magazine-clarkesworld-artificial-intelligence)). Its editor calls detection tools "unreliable" ([Quartz](https://qz.com/clarkesword-neil-clarke-chatgpt-ai-q-and-a-1850144881)). Funders are reacting too: NIH notice NOT-OD-25-132 (July 2025) restricts AI-developed applications and caps applications per investigator ([CASRAI, secondary](https://casrai.org/news/ai-grant-application-checklist)). Calls now carry AI policies, attestations and caps that applicants must read before submitting. Chill Subs added per-magazine AI-policy fields in 2026 ([Chill Subs, Apr 2026](https://www.chillsubs.com/blog/april-2026-roundup)). For organizations, a portal fed by a verified-artist audience is a partial answer to spam.

**Writers demand consent for AI training.** **96% of 2,400+ authors** told the Authors Guild that consent and payment should be required ([Authors Guild, Dec 2023](https://authorsguild.org/news/ag-ai-survey-reveals-authors-overwhelmingly-want-consent-and-compensation-for-use-of-their-works/)). **98%** of Australian Society of Authors respondents agreed ([ASA, 2025, search excerpt](https://www.asauthors.org.au/?p=21536)). In the UK, **26% of illustrators and 36% of translators** had already lost work to generative AI ([Books+Publishing, 15 Apr 2024](https://www.booksandpublishing.com.au/articles/2024/04/15/250317/a-third-of-translators-report-losing-work-due-to-ai/)). Missa never handles the work itself, so a "nothing trains on your work" promise is cheap to keep and speaks to a near-universal concern.

**Funding cuts are increasing churn among venues.** In May 2025 the NEA sent termination or withdrawal letters to **all 51 Literary Arts grantees**, according to CLMP ([LA Review of Books](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/we-dont-have-any-reserves/)); Publishers Weekly counted at least 38 CLMP members with over $1.2M at stake ([PW, 12 May 2025](https://www.publishersweekly.com/pw/print/20250512/97702-nea-literary-grants-staff-cut-as-trump-proposes-eliminating-the-agency.html)). The NEA also cancelled its FY2026 Creative Writing Fellowships ([NPR, 26 Aug 2025](https://www.npr.org/2025/08/26/nx-s1-5518202/nea-cancels-creative-writing-fellowship)). Creative Scotland closed its Open Fund for Individuals over a £6.6M gap ([Irish News/PA, 2024](https://www.irishnews.com/entertainment/creative-scotlands-hands-tied-when-key-fund-was-closed-chief-executive-says-TWPFLOEIB5K2TFZ7B45BENUBQI/)). At the same time, launches hold near 200 a year. More venues, less stable ones, and fees as a response to lost grants all raise the value of a source that rechecks calls. They also squeeze the budgets of the organizations Missa wants to sell to.

**Creators now pay for subscriptions, but as fans more than as buyers of tools.** Substack passed **5 million paid subscriptions in March 2025** ([Tubefilter, 12 Mar 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/03/12/substack-five-million-paid-subscribers-journalist-reporter-newsletter/)). Patreon's survey found **53% of creators say followers are harder to reach than five years ago** and **81% want a direct channel** ([Tubefilter, 19 Feb 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/19/patreon-state-of-create-report-fans-followers/)). Subscription paying is normal in writing culture. No public data measures how many writers pay for submission tools.

**Discovery is moving to AI answers and newsletters.** Zero-click news searches rose from **56% to about 69%** after AI Overviews launched, while ChatGPT referrals to news sites grew from under 1M to over **25M** in a year ([TechCrunch, 2 Jul 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/07/02/chatgpt-referrals-to-news-sites-are-growing-but-not-enough-to-offset-search-declines)). ChatGPT accounted for **81.7% of AI referral traffic** to the top 1,000 sites in June 2025 ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/chatgpt-now-accounts-for-80percent-of-ai-traffic-to-top-websites)). A directory with clean, citable, source-linked pages for each call is the kind of page assistants cite. Missa's 605,720 stored source pages and its per-call organizer links suit this channel. A Google-only SEO strategy is a weaker bet than it was in 2023.

## Positioning and pricing follow from Duotrope's repricing and Chill Subs' growth

### Keep Plus at $6/$60, but sell on what Duotrope lacks

Duotrope's move to **$6/$60** removed Missa's price advantage. It also validated the price point: an incumbent with 13 years of subscribers judged that writers will bear it. Undercutting to $5/$50 would signal a cheaper Duotrope and give up roughly 17% of revenue. The data does not suggest price is what blocks adoption. Missa should hold $6/$60 and lead with four contrasts, each traceable to evidence above:

1. **A free tier.** Duotrope offers only a 10-day trial ([Duotrope FAQ](https://duotrope.com/about/faq.aspx)), and the 2013 backlash shows writers punish paywalls.
2. **Breadth across disciplines.** Residencies, grants and fellowships sit beside magazines. No competitor covers writers and visual artists equally.
3. **Source-linked, current data.** This directly answers the old "paying to feed a wiki" complaint, provided the 17% 30-day recheck rate and the 45% unknown-fee share improve first.
4. **Fee, waiver and AI-policy fields.** These are decision data in a market where 56% of the top 100 magazines charge fees.

Against Chill Subs, Missa Plus at **$60 a year is 37.5% cheaper than Chill Subs Pro at $96**. Chill Subs Pro, though, bundles community, reviews and coaching, which Missa does not match. The honest framing is "Chill Subs for everyone who isn't only a writer." Missa should not try to out-community Chill Subs.

Three pricing adjustments are worth testing:

- **Annual-first checkout.** The annual plan is the realistic ARPU anchor.
- **A no-questions fee scholarship or regional price**, following the roughly 10% scholarship uptake at Chill Subs and Duotrope's INR and NZD price lists.
- **Paid matching or curated lists**, given that Chill Subs sells human-curated lists at $50–$150. Missa's messaging rules forbid predicting acceptance, so such a feature must be described as fit to what someone makes, never as chances of success.

For visual artists, the evidence favours keeping the creator side free and charging the organizations that run calls, as CaFE, Zealous and EntryThingy already do.

### Price the organization portal against Submittable's take rate, not its subscription

Small organizations pay **$0–$80 a month**, say they object to Submittable's **$0.99 + 5%**, slow payouts and opaque pricing, and already have cheap literary-native alternatives (Duosuma at about $0.09 per submission; Zealous capped at £2 commission). A credible Missa portal would offer:

- a free tier sized for a single small call
- **published, flat pricing** in the $20–$60-a-month band for magazines
- per-call pricing for residencies, set below CaFE's $250 a call
- a fee take well below Submittable's, so that a $3 fee nets the magazine materially more than $1.86

The wedge that no portal vendor has is distribution. Missa can tell an organization: "your call is already on Missa, and here is who saved it." That loop depends on organization-claim routes, which are switched off today, and on per-listing view and save metrics, which do not yet exist (Internal, from the earlier pitch pack). Those two features are the prerequisite for the organization SOM above.

## Conclusion

A creator subscription is a strong retention product but a weak business model. The paying pool across all writer tools is probably only **$1–6M a year**, and the two incumbents in it have just raised prices instead of growing users. Missa's best asset is its source-linked catalogue of **2,526 organizations**. That catalogue can do three jobs. It is the free hook for creators. It is the sales list for an organization portal priced against Submittable's take rate. It is also the kind of structured, citable data AI assistants need as discovery shifts away from search. The pitch should therefore treat Plus as proof of willingness to pay, and treat the organization side and payments as the path to scale.

Three things will change this picture fastest, and each can be measured within months:

1. The first paying Plus members, and their conversion rate against the 5% benchmark.
2. Turning on organization claims, plus per-listing view and save metrics, so "your call is already on Missa" can be demonstrated.
3. Raising data freshness above Duotrope's monthly recheck and filling the 45% of unknown fees, so that the transparency claim survives scrutiny.

Until then, investors and press should be shown the catalogue and the market gap, and told plainly that traction has not started.
